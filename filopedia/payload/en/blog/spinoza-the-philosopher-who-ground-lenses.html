Spinoza, the philosopher who ground lenses

24 November 2026 · 5 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

Born in Amsterdam on 24 November 1632, he was cast out of his community and wrote an ethics as if it were geometry.

On 24 November 1632, Baruch Spinoza was born in Amsterdam. He became one of the most original and most modest philosophers in history. He lived simply, grinding lenses for microscopes and telescopes, and wrote a body of work that is still studied and debated today.

Cast out of his community

Spinoza came from a family of Sephardic Jews of Portuguese origin who had taken refuge in the Netherlands, one of the most tolerant places in Europe at the time. He studied at the community school, but soon began asking awkward questions about the Bible and about God.

In 1656, aged 23, the Amsterdam synagogue expelled him with a very harsh ban, the herem. The exact reasons are not well known, but they had to do with his ideas. From then on he lived apart, in several Dutch towns, with few friends and a great deal of correspondence.

An ethics written like geometry

His main work is the Ethics, Demonstrated in Geometrical Order. As the title says, it is written like a geometry book, with definitions, axioms and propositions proved one from another. Spinoza wanted philosophy to be as rigorous as mathematics, an aim he shared with Descartes and with rationalism.

His most famous idea is that God and nature are the same thing: Deus sive Natura. God would not be a person who rewards and punishes, but the whole of reality. To some this was atheism; to others, a deep form of religious feeling.

Understanding in order to be free

For Spinoza, we suffer because we are carried away by passions such as fear, envy or anger. Freedom does not mean doing whatever we want, but understanding why we feel what we feel. The better we understand the causes, the less they rule us.

He died in The Hague in February 1677, aged only 44, probably of a lung disease that the glass dust from his work may have worsened. The Ethics was published that same year, after his death, as he had wished.
