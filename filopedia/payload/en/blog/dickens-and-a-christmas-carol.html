Dickens and «A Christmas Carol»

22 December 2026 · 5 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

In 1843 Charles Dickens wrote the story of Scrooge in six weeks, and changed the way Christmas is celebrated.

In October 1843, Charles Dickens began writing a Christmas story. He finished it in about six weeks and it was published on 19 December. The first 6,000 copies had sold out by Christmas Eve. It was A Christmas Carol, and almost two centuries later the story of Scrooge comes back every December.

A boy in a factory

Dickens was born in Portsmouth in 1812. When he was twelve, his father was imprisoned for debt and the boy had to work in a blacking factory, sticking labels on pots. The experience marked him for life and explains why his novels are full of poor, orphaned and exploited children, as in Oliver Twist or David Copperfield.

Scrooge and the three spirits

Ebenezer Scrooge is an old London miser who despises Christmas. On Christmas Eve he is visited by the ghost of his former partner, Marley, who announces that three spirits will come: the Ghost of Christmas Past, of Christmas Present and of Christmas Yet to Come. With them, Scrooge relives his youth, sees the humble home of his clerk Bob Cratchit and his sick son, Tiny Tim, and looks on his own death with nobody to mourn him. When he wakes up, he is a different man.

How it changed Christmas

Dickens wrote the story at a time of great poverty in England's industrial cities. He wanted to stir his readers' conscience about those in need. The book helped spread the idea of Christmas as a family celebration of generosity, with dinner, presents and good wishes.

Dickens read the story in public many times, to great acclaim. Other great novels followed, such as A Tale of Two Cities and Great Expectations. He died in 1870 and is buried in Westminster Abbey.
