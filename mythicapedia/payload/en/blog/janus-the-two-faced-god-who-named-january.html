Janus, the two-faced god who gave January its name

29 December 2026 · 5 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

One face looks to the past and the other to the future: the Roman god of doors and beginnings.

January is the month of beginnings, of resolutions and of looking back to take stock. We owe its name to a god who did exactly that: Janus, the two-faced Roman god, with one face looking to the past and the other to the future.

The god of doorways

Janus was the god of doors, passages and beginnings. His name is related to «ianua», Latin for door. That is why he had two faces: a door opens both outwards and inwards. He was called upon at the start of anything, and in prayers his name was spoken before that of any other god, even before Jupiter.

A purely Roman god

Unlike many Roman gods, who had a Greek counterpart, Janus had no equivalent in Greece. He was a truly Italic god. According to legend, he was an ancient king of Latium who welcomed Saturn when he fled from heaven, and who received from him the gift of seeing both the past and the future.

The gates of war

In the Roman Forum there was a small temple of Janus with two doors. When Rome was at war, they stayed open; when there was peace throughout the empire, they were closed. Since Rome was almost always at war somewhere, they were closed very few times in its history. The emperor Augustus boasted of having closed them three times.

The first day of the year

The month of «Ianuarius», January, was dedicated to Janus. The poet Ovid describes in his Fasti how the Romans celebrated 1 January: they exchanged good wishes and small gifts, such as dates, figs and honey, so that the year would begin sweetly, and coins so that it would be prosperous. People tried not to quarrel that day, because what was done at the start set the tone for the rest of the year.

Why the year starts in January

The oldest Roman calendar began in March. That is why September, October, November and December have those names: they were the seventh, eighth, ninth and tenth months. From 153 BC, the consuls began to take office on 1 January, and that date eventually became the start of the year.

Janus today

As well as in the name of the month, Janus lives on in the phrase «two-faced» and in a moon of Saturn, Janus, discovered in 1966. He is the perfect god to start the year with: looking back without forgetting what we have learned, and looking ahead with enthusiasm.
