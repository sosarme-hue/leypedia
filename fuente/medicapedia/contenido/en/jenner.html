For centuries {d:viruela|smallpox} was one of the most feared diseases in the world. It killed a large share of those who caught it and left many survivors scarred or blind. In May 1796 an English country doctor named {p:edward-jenner|Edward Jenner} carried out an experiment that would change the history of medicine.

{fig:img/p/edward-jenner.jpg|p/edward-jenner.html|Edward Jenner, painted by James Northcote. Image: Wikimedia Commons.}

A curious country doctor

Jenner was born in 1749 in Berkeley, in the English county of Gloucestershire. He trained in London under the surgeon {p:john-hunter|John Hunter}, who taught him not to settle for thinking but to test things by experiment. Then he went home to work as a country doctor.

There was a belief in the countryside: milkmaids who had caught cowpox, a mild disease that produced sores on the hands, did not later catch human smallpox. Jenner decided to put it to the test.

The 1796 experiment

On 14 May 1796 he took material from a sore on the hand of Sarah Nelmes, a milkmaid infected by a cow called Blossom, and introduced it through small cuts into the arm of James Phipps, the eight-year-old son of his gardener. The boy had a slight fever and felt unwell, but soon recovered. Weeks later Jenner exposed him to human smallpox, and the boy did not fall ill.

Such an experiment would be unthinkable today, but at the time variolation was already practised: people were inoculated with human smallpox to cause a mild form of the disease. It was a risky method, and Jenner's was much safer.

The birth of a word

In 1798 Jenner published his results. He called the cow material variolae vaccinae, «smallpox of the cow», and that is where the word vaccine comes from. At first there was mockery, and cartoons showed vaccinated people sprouting cows' heads, but vaccination spread very quickly through Europe and the world. Spain took it to the Americas and Asia with the {p:francisco-javier-balmis|Balmis Expedition}.

A disease eradicated

Almost two centuries later, in 1980, the World Health Organization declared smallpox eradicated. It is the only human disease ever wiped out completely, and it all began with the curiosity of a country doctor and a milkmaid.
