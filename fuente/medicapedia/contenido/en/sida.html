Every 1 December is World AIDS Day, created in 1988 to remember the victims and to fight stigma. We look back at the history of an epidemic that in forty years has gone from a death sentence to a chronic condition that can be controlled with treatment.

The first cases

On 5 June 1981 the US Centers for Disease Control published a short report on five young men in Los Angeles with a rare kind of pneumonia, caused by Pneumocystis. More cases soon appeared, with unusual infections and tumours such as Kaposi's sarcoma. All of them had a destroyed immune system. In 1982 the disease got its name: acquired immunodeficiency syndrome, AIDS.

Finding the virus

In 1983 a team at the Pasteur Institute in Paris, including Françoise Barré-Sinoussi and Luc Montagnier, isolated the virus responsible, later named HIV, the human immunodeficiency virus. Both received the Nobel Prize in Medicine in 2008. It became clear that the virus was transmitted sexually, through blood and from mother to child, and in 1985 the first blood test to detect it arrived.

The hardest years

During the eighties and early nineties AIDS killed hundreds of thousands of people, many of them young. It hit gay men, people who injected drugs and people who received transfusions especially hard. Fear and prejudice meant that many patients also suffered rejection and discrimination. Well-known figures such as Rock Hudson and Freddie Mercury died of the disease.

From AZT to combination therapy

In 1987 the first drug against HIV, zidovudine or AZT, was approved. The big change came in 1996, when combination antiretroviral therapy was presented, mixing several drugs to hold the virus back. Mortality plummeted in countries that had access to it.

A fight that goes on

Today a person with HIV who follows their treatment can have a life expectancy similar to that of the general population, and if the virus is undetectable in their blood they do not pass it on sexually. But access to treatment is still unequal around the world, and there is still no vaccine. That is why 1 December is still a necessary date.
