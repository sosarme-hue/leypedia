On 16 May 1929, at the Roosevelt Hotel in Hollywood, some 270 people attended a dinner at which the awards of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences were presented for the first time. The winners had been known for months and the presentation took about fifteen minutes. That is how the history of the Oscars began.

{fig:img/p/Q272036.webp|p/alas-1927.html|Poster for Wings (1927), the first winner of the top award. Poster: TMDB.}

An Academy for labour peace

The Academy was founded in 1927 at the initiative of Louis B. Mayer, head of Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer. Its original purpose had more to do with relations between the studios and their workers than with art. The awards were a way of giving the industry prestige.

The first winners

The first film to win was {p:alas-1927|Wings}, by William A. Wellman, a story of First World War pilots. It was the only silent film to win the top prize until The Artist, in 2012. That year there was also a parallel award, for «Unique and Artistic Production», which went to F. W. Murnau's Sunrise. The first acting award went to the German Emil Jannings.

Why «Oscar»?

The statuette, a knight holding a sword on a reel of film, was designed by Cedric Gibbons, MGM's art director. Where the nickname comes from is unclear. The most repeated story says that an Academy employee, Margaret Herrick, remarked that it looked like her uncle Oscar. The Academy officially adopted the name in 1939.

From radio to television

The ceremony was first broadcast on radio in 1930 and on television in 1953. It has since become the great night of cinema, with records such as the eleven Oscars won by Ben-Hur, Titanic and The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King.
