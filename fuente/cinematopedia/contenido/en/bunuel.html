{n:luis-bunuel|Luis Buñuel} was born in Calanda, in the Spanish province of Teruel, in 1900. He remembered the Holy Week drums of his home town as one of the sounds of his childhood, and that mixture of religion, ritual and rebellion runs through all his work. For many, he is the most important Spanish film-maker in history.

{fig:img/r/Q51545.webp|n/luis-bunuel.html|Luis Buñuel.}

Paris and surrealism

As a young man he lived at the Residencia de Estudiantes in Madrid, where he became friends with Federico García Lorca and Salvador Dalí. He then moved to Paris, and there, in 1929, he made Un chien andalou with Dalí, a silent short that begins with a razor slicing an eye. The surrealists hailed it as a masterpiece. The following year came {p:la-edad-de-oro-1930|L'Âge d'or}, so provocative that it was banned after the disturbances at its screenings.

Exile and Mexico

After the Spanish Civil War he went into exile, first in the United States and then in Mexico. There, in the fifties, he made films for a popular audience, but also personal works such as {p:los-olvidados-1950|Los olvidados} (1950), about the children of the capital's slums, which won him the Best Director award at Cannes.

Viridiana, the Palme d'Or and the scandal

In 1961 he returned to Spain to make {p:viridiana-1961|Viridiana}. It won the Palme d'Or at Cannes, the only one for a Spanish film, but the Vatican condemned it and Franco's regime banned it. The famous scene of the beggars imitating Leonardo's Last Supper is one of the best-known images in his cinema.

The last years in France

In his final years he worked mainly in France, with films such as {p:belle-de-jour-1967|Belle de jour}, {p:tristana-1970|Tristana}, shot in Toledo, and {p:el-discreto-encanto-de-la-burguesia-1972|The Discreet Charm of the Bourgeoisie}, which won the Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film. He died in Mexico City in 1983. In his memoirs, My Last Sigh, he wrote that he was an atheist «thank God».
