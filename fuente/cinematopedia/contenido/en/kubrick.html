In April 1968 a science fiction film opened that began with apes discovering a tool and ended with a foetus floating in space. In between there was hardly any dialogue. Many viewers came out baffled. Today, {p:2001-una-odisea-del-espacio-1968|2001: A Space Odyssey} is considered one of the most influential films ever made.

{fig:img/r/Q2001.webp|n/stanley-kubrick.html|Stanley Kubrick.}

A director who left nothing to chance

{n:stanley-kubrick|Stanley Kubrick} was born in New York in 1928. He started as a photographer for Look magazine, which is where his obsession with the image came from. Before 2001 he had already made {p:senderos-de-gloria-1957|Paths of Glory}, {p:espartaco-1960|Spartacus}, {p:lolita-1962|Lolita} and {p:dr-strangelove-o-como-aprendi-a-no-preocuparme-y-a-amar-la-bomba-1964|Dr. Strangelove}. He was known for shooting dozens of takes and for controlling every detail of his films.

A project with Arthur C. Clarke

For 2001 he teamed up with the British writer Arthur C. Clarke. They started from one of Clarke's short stories, The Sentinel, and worked on the screenplay and the novel at the same time; the book came out shortly after the film. Kubrick consulted scientists and NASA so that everything would look plausible, a year before humans reached the Moon.

Special effects without computers

The visual effects, supervised in part by Douglas Trumbull, were made with models, projections and camera tricks. The rotating ship in which the astronauts jog in circles was a huge set that really did turn. The film won the only Oscar of Kubrick's career, for its visual effects.

HAL 9000 and the music

The film's most memorable character is a computer: HAL 9000, the calm-voiced artificial intelligence that turns against the crew. And its soundtrack changed the way films use music: Kubrick dropped the score he had commissioned and used classical works instead, such as Richard Strauss's Also sprach Zarathustra and Johann Strauss's The Blue Danube.

Kubrick went on to make {p:la-naranja-mecanica-1971|A Clockwork Orange}, {p:barry-lyndon-1975|Barry Lyndon} and {p:el-resplandor-1980|The Shining}, but 2001 is still the film that best sums up his ambition.
