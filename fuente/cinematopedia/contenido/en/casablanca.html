When Warner Bros. began shooting Casablanca in May 1942, nobody at the studio thought they were making history. It was one more of the many productions that left Burbank every year, based on a play that had never even been staged: Everybody Comes to Rick's, by Murray Burnett and Joan Alison. It opened in New York on 26 November 1942. Eighty-four years later, it is still one of the best-loved films of all time.

A script written on the go

The script passed through several hands: the twins Julius and Philip Epstein, Howard Koch and, uncredited, Casey Robinson. It was rewritten during the shoot, and the actors often got their pages on the day. Legend has it that {n:ingrid-bergman|Ingrid Bergman} kept asking which of the two men Ilsa should end up with, and nobody could tell her. Perhaps that is why her gaze, caught between {n:humphrey-bogart|Bogart} and {n:paul-henreid|Paul Henreid}, feels so ambiguous.

A cast of refugees

The film was made in the middle of the Second World War, and many of its actors were Europeans who had fled Nazism. {n:conrad-veidt|Conrad Veidt}, who plays Major Strasser, had left Germany. {n:peter-lorre|Peter Lorre}, {n:curt-bois|Curt Bois} and {n:madeleine-lebeau|Madeleine Lebeau} were exiles too. That is why the scene in which the whole café sings La Marseillaise to drown out the German officers feels so genuine: many of those crying on screen knew exactly what it meant.

The all-round director

In charge was {n:michael-curtiz|Michael Curtiz}, a tireless Hungarian who made several films a year and was equally at home with drama, adventure or musicals. The music was by {n:max-steiner|Max Steiner}, who built the score around an existing song, As Time Goes By, and the French anthem.

Lines everyone knows

Few films have left so many lines in our collective memory: «We'll always have Paris», «Play it, Sam» (it is never actually «Play it again, Sam»), «This is the beginning of a beautiful friendship». Casablanca won three Oscars in 1944, including Best Picture, and it has appeared on lists of the greatest films ever made ever since.

The most surprising thing is that none of this was planned. It was the result of a good studio working at full speed, an exceptional cast and a historical moment that gave meaning to every scene. If you have never seen it, our page on {p:casablanca-1942|Casablanca} is a good place to start.
