Few films are as closely linked to Christmas as {p:que-bello-es-vivir-1946|It's a Wonderful Life}. Every December it returns to television screens around the world with the story of George Bailey, the man who, about to give up, learns from an angel what his town would have been like if he had never been born. The odd thing is that when it came out, it was a flop.

{fig:img/p/Q204191.webp|p/que-bello-es-vivir-1946.html|Poster for It's a Wonderful Life (1946). Poster: TMDB.}

A Christmas story

The film was based on a short story, The Greatest Gift, which Philip Van Doren Stern had sent to his friends as a Christmas card in 1943. {n:frank-capra|Frank Capra}, just back from the Second World War, where he had made documentaries for the army, chose it as the first film for his new company, Liberty Films.

For George he cast {n:james-stewart|James Stewart}, who was also back from the war, where he had been a bomber pilot. Alongside him were {n:donna-reed|Donna Reed}, {n:lionel-barrymore|Lionel Barrymore} as the greedy Mr Potter, and {n:henry-travers|Henry Travers} as Clarence, the angel second class who has not yet earned his wings.

From flop to classic

It opened in December 1946 and the box office did not cover its high cost. It received five Oscar nominations but won none. For years it seemed forgotten. Everything changed in 1974, when its copyright lapsed in the United States through an oversight and television channels could show it for free. They began to show it every Christmas, and a whole generation grew up with it.

Capra said it was his favourite film, and James Stewart felt the same about his own role.

Other films for the season

Christmas cinema has other classics. Capra had already triumphed with comedies like {p:sucedio-una-noche-1934|It Happened One Night}, and the forties brought films such as Holiday Inn (1942), in which Bing Crosby first sang White Christmas, and Miracle on 34th Street (1947), with Santa Claus put on trial. But none has struck a chord like the story of George Bailey, which reminds us that no life is small.
