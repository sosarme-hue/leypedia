The meal is the part of the wedding guests remember most, and also the biggest item in the budget. Choosing the caterer and the menu well is essential. Here are the keys.

Venue caterer or outside caterer?

Many {u:hotels/|hotels}, {u:restaurants/|restaurants} and {u:farmhouses-and-estates/|estates} have their own kitchen and don't allow outside caterers. It's the simplest option. If the venue allows it, you can choose an independent {u:catering/|caterer}, with more freedom but a bit more organising: kitchen, set-up, staff and tableware.

Meal formats

  • Seated dinner: the classic, with starter, main and dessert. Elegant and orderly.
  • Long cocktail reception: no fixed tables, lots of small dishes. More lively and social.
  • Buffet or food stations: guests help themselves. Good variety and a relaxed feel.
  • Mixed: a long drinks reception followed by a served main course.

Questions for the caterer

  • What does the price per person include? Drinks, bread, coffee, cake?
  • How many waiters per table?
  • Is there a children's menu and a menu for suppliers?
  • How are allergies and special diets handled?
  • Is a tasting included? For how many people?
  • How much is the open bar, and for how many hours?

The tasting

Do it two or three months before the wedding. Pay attention to flavour, but also to portions, temperature and presentation. This is the moment to change dishes.

Tips for choosing the menu

  • Think about the season: fresh dishes in summer, something warming in winter.
  • A generous drinks reception is worth more than many courses.
  • Include a vegetarian option for everyone.
  • Local produce, which is often cheaper too.
  • For dessert, you can pair the cake with a sweet table. Find ideas in {u:cakes-and-pastry/|cakes and pastry}.
