New Year's Eve is the party night of the year, and the hardest to organise: everywhere is full, everything costs more and someone always wants something different. If you're celebrating as a group this year, these ideas will help you decide.

Dinner and party at a restaurant or hall

Many {u:restaurants/|restaurants}, {u:hotels/|hotels} and {u:banquet-and-party-halls/|party halls} prepare a special menu with the traditional grapes, party favours and dancing until dawn. It's the easiest option: nobody cooks or washes up. At this point in the year, book as soon as you can.

A house or farmhouse for the group

Renting a country house or a {u:farmhouses-and-estates/|farmhouse} for a few days is ideal for large groups of friends or families. You cook together, celebrate without a curfew and nobody has to drive home.

At home, but different

  • Hire a {u:catering/|caterer} or a private chef so nobody spends the night in the kitchen.
  • Choose a theme or a dress code: the roaring twenties, all gold, black tie.
  • Prepare games for before midnight: quizzes, karaoke, a review of the year in photos.
  • A small {u:sound-and-lighting/|sound and lighting} set turns the living room into a dance floor.

Things to decide in advance

  • Budget per person and how costs are shared.
  • Getting home. It's the busiest night of the year for taxis. Arrange designated drivers or a coach.
  • Children. If there are children, plan a space for them and what happens when they fall asleep.
  • The grapes. In Spain, it's tradition to eat twelve grapes on the twelve chimes of midnight. Someone needs to buy them and make sure the TV or radio works at midnight.

One last tip

New Year's Eve has a reputation for disappointing because expectations are so high. What works best is simple: good company, good food and someone in charge of making sure everything is ready.
