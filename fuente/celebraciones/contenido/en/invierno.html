Most weddings take place between May and October. But more and more couples are choosing winter, and not just for the price. A wedding in December, January or February has its own charm: warm light, blankets, fireplaces and a much more intimate feel.

The advantages

  • Price. Many venues and suppliers have off-season rates, sometimes much lower than in summer.
  • Availability. It's far easier to get the venue, photographer or date you want.
  • Guests. Nobody is on holiday, and there are no clashes with other summer weddings.
  • No heat. No soaked suits or melting make-up.

Things to bear in mind

  • Less daylight. In December it gets dark early. Plan the ceremony and photos in daylight, or embrace the night with good lighting.
  • The weather. Choose an indoor, heated venue. {u:hotels/|Hotels}, {u:banquet-and-party-halls/|banquet halls} and {u:farmhouses-and-estates/|farmhouses} with an indoor hall are the safe bet.
  • The holidays. Close to Christmas, guests have other plans and travel costs more. Let them know well in advance.

Ideas for a winter wedding

  • Lots of warm light: candles, fairy lights and ambient {u:sound-and-lighting/|lighting}.
  • Deep colours: burgundy, bottle green, midnight blue, gold.
  • Seasonal {u:flowers-and-decor/|flowers}, branches, pine cones and eucalyptus.
  • Blankets and shawls for guests during the outdoor moments.
  • Warming seasonal dishes, with a hot chocolate or roasted chestnut corner.
  • A bridal cape or coat: it keeps you warm and looks great in photos.

And the party?

In winter the party tends to be longer and livelier: there's no waiting for the sun to set to start dancing. A good {u:music-and-entertainment/|music and entertainment} team does the rest.
