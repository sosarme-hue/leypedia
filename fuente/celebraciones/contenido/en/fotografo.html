Flowers wilt, the cake gets eaten and the dress is put away. What remains of a wedding are the photos and the video. That's why choosing the photographer is one of the most important decisions. Here's how to get it right.

First, the style

Every photographer works differently. Some prefer a natural, almost documentary approach with no posing. Others focus on posed portraits and composition. Some use vivid colours, others soft tones or black and white. Look at lots of weddings and decide what you like before asking for quotes.

Ask to see full weddings

Any photographer will show their ten best shots. Ask to see one or two complete weddings from start to finish: that shows how they handle tricky moments, such as a dark hall or a ceremony in harsh sunlight.

Questions to ask

  • How many hours are covered? From getting ready to the first dance?
  • How many edited photos will we get, and how soon?
  • Is there a second photographer?
  • Is a printed album included? An engagement or post-wedding shoot?
  • What happens if you are ill on the day?
  • How are the photos delivered, and how long are they kept?

What about video?

Many couples who hesitated about video are grateful for it years later: it captures voices, speeches and atmosphere. On a tight budget, a highlights film of a few minutes may be enough. Some studios offer photo and video with a coordinated team.

Chemistry matters

The photographer will be with you all day, in your most intimate moments. Meet them or have a video call before booking. If you feel comfortable, it will show in the photos.
