The budget is the first big decision of any wedding, and the one that prevents the most arguments if done well. You don't need to be a finance expert: just be realistic, write everything down and leave room for the unexpected.

Step 1: how much can we spend?

Before looking at venues, set a total figure. Count what you have saved, what you can save before the wedding and what family will contribute, if anything. Talk about it openly and early.

Step 2: the number of guests

This has the biggest effect on the final price: almost everything is charged per person. Ten more guests can make a noticeable difference. Make one list of must-haves and another of "if possible".

Step 3: split the money

There are no fixed rules, but a rough split could look like this:

  • Venue and food: around half of the total.
  • Photography and video: about 10%.
  • Outfits and beauty: about 10%.
  • Music, flowers and decor: about 10 to 15%.
  • Invitations, favours, rings, transport and other: the rest.
  • Contingency: set aside 5 to 10%.

Adjust the percentages to what matters most to you: if photography is your priority, cut back elsewhere.

Step 4: get quotes and compare them properly

Get at least three quotes for each major supplier. When comparing, check what each includes: VAT, hours, travel, open bar, set-up, overtime. A low price can hide many extras. On celebraciones.org you can {u:get-quotes/|ask several suppliers for quotes} at once.

Step 5: write it all down

A simple spreadsheet with three columns, planned, actual and paid, avoids surprises. Note the due dates for deposits and instalments too.

Ways to save without it showing

  • Marry off-season, on a Friday or a Sunday. We cover this in {art:invierno|winter weddings}.
  • Choose a venue that includes catering, furniture and decor.
  • Use seasonal flowers.
  • Limit the open bar to a set number of hours.
  • Replace guest favours with a charity donation.
