After Christmas Eve dinner and Christmas lunch there is almost always something left over: some turkey, half a lamb, stock, seafood, turrón. Traditional Spanish cooking has wonderful recipes to make the most of it all. Here are the classics and a few ideas.

{fig:img/p/Q70125.webp|p/croqueta.html|Homemade croquettes. Photo: Jonathan Pincas (CC BY 2.0), Wikimedia Commons.}

Croquettes, queens of leftovers

{p:croqueta|Croquettes} were born precisely to use up leftovers. The oldest known recipe is French, from 1691, but they became popular in Spain in the 19th century, and by the early 20th century cookbooks already included meat, fish and vegetable versions. Turkey, chicken, ham, stew or salt cod: almost any chopped leftover mixed with a thick béchamel becomes a croquette.

Saint Stephen's cannelloni

In Catalonia, on 26 December people eat cannelloni filled with the meat from the Christmas escudella and roast, minced and mixed with béchamel. It is one of the best-loved leftover recipes in Catalan cooking.

Ropa vieja and pringá

The remains of the {p:cocido-madrileno|cocido} give us ropa vieja, shredded meat sautéed with peppers and tomato, and in Andalusia pringá, the meat and pork fat from the stew mashed and served in bread.

Stocks and soups

Turkey or capon bones and seafood shells make an excellent stock. Use it for a picadillo soup, a soupy rice or a fish soup the next evening.

Sweets that transform

Soft turrón can be melted into milk for a shake or added to homemade ice cream. Yesterday's roscón and {p:panettone|panettone} make French toast or a bread pudding, and crumbled marzipan gives a special touch to a sponge cake.

Safety tips

Into the fridge quickly. Leftovers should not sit more than two hours at room temperature.

Three or four days. That is a reasonable time frame for eating most chilled cooked leftovers.

Freeze in portions. Stock, shredded meat and unfried croquettes freeze very well.
