Every European country has its Christmas sweet, and many are prepared over weeks, like a ritual. Join us on a trip through the continent's Christmas shop windows, from Germany to Greece.

{fig:img/g/Q106252-1.webp|p/mazapan.html|Marzipan figures for New Year's Eve in Salzburg. Photo: Reykholt (CC BY-SA 3.0), Wikimedia Commons.}

Germany: stollen and lebkuchen

Stollen is a long sweet bread with raisins, candied fruit, almonds and sometimes a marzipan core, covered in butter and icing sugar. Its shape is said to recall the Christ Child wrapped in swaddling clothes. Dresden's is the most famous. Nuremberg's lebkuchen are soft honey and spice biscuits, relatives of gingerbread.

Italy: panettone and pandoro

Milan gave us {p:panettone|panettone}, tall and full of fruit, and Verona gave us pandoro, shaped like an eight-pointed star, with no fruit and dusted with sugar. In Naples, struffoli are small fried balls coated in honey.

France: the bûche de Noël

The Yule log is a rolled sponge cake covered in chocolate cream to imitate bark. It recalls the tradition of burning a large log in the hearth during the holidays.

United Kingdom: Christmas pudding and mince pies

Christmas pudding is made weeks ahead with dried fruit, spices, suet and brandy; tradition says the whole family should stir it and make a wish. Mince pies are small tarts filled with spiced dried fruit.

Netherlands and Belgium: speculaas and banket

For Saint Nicholas, speculaas biscuits with cinnamon and clove are baked, along with puff pastry letters filled with almond paste.

Scandinavia: gingerbread and saffron buns

In Sweden, pepparkakor gingerbread and lussekatter, S-shaped saffron buns, are eaten above all on Saint Lucy's Day, 13 December.

Greece: melomakarona and kourabiedes

Melomakarona are olive oil, orange and honey biscuits with walnuts; kourabiedes are butter and almond biscuits covered in icing sugar, very similar to Spanish polvorones.

Portugal: bolo rei

Bolo rei is the Portuguese version of the {p:roscon-de-reyes|roscón}, with candied fruit and nuts, eaten from Christmas to Epiphany.
