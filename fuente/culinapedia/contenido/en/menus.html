Christmas is celebrated almost everywhere, but every country brings it to the table in its own way. There are Christmas Eve dinners of fish and dinners of meat, lunches on 25 December and celebrations that last until Epiphany. Here is a trip around the world in ten Christmas tables.

Spain: seafood, lamb and turrón

In {u:paises/espana.html|Spain} the big meal is Christmas Eve dinner, on the night of the 24th. Depending on the region, the star is seafood, roast lamb or suckling pig, sea bream or salt cod. In Catalonia, escudella i carn d'olla, a hearty stew, is traditional on Christmas Day, and in many places the cannelloni of St Stephen's Day, the 26th, use up the leftover meat. For dessert there is {p:turron|turrón}, {p:mazapan|marzipan} and polvorones, crumbly almond shortbreads.

Italy: seven fishes and panettone

In southern {u:paises/italia.html|Italy}, Christmas Eve is a day of abstinence and fish is eaten for dinner: eel in Naples, salt cod, clams. Among Italian Americans this became the "Feast of the Seven Fishes". On the 25th come stuffed pastas, such as tortellini in broth from Emilia-Romagna, and to finish, Milanese {p:panettone|panettone} or pandoro from Verona.

France: réveillon and the bûche

In France, the dinner on the 24th is called réveillon and usually includes oysters, foie gras, smoked salmon and turkey or capon stuffed with chestnuts. The essential dessert is the bûche de Noël, a rolled sponge shaped like a log, a reminder of the old custom of burning a log in the hearth over the holidays. In Provence there are also the thirteen desserts, in honour of Jesus and the twelve apostles.

United Kingdom: turkey and Christmas pudding

The British feast is lunch on the 25th: roast turkey with cranberry sauce, roast potatoes, Brussels sprouts and pigs in blankets. The finale is Christmas pudding, a pudding of dried fruit and spices made weeks in advance, doused in brandy and carried to the table in flames.

Germany: goose and stollen

In Germany roast goose with red cabbage and potato dumplings is traditional, although Christmas Eve is often simpler, sometimes sausages and potato salad. All through Advent people eat spiced biscuits and stollen, a sweet bread with candied fruit and marzipan dusted with icing sugar, typical of Dresden.

Poland: the twelve-dish supper

The Polish Wigilia, on the 24th, begins when the first star appears. On the table are twelve meatless dishes, including beetroot soup with tiny dumplings, cabbage and mushroom pierogi, and carp, which many families buy alive and keep in the bathtub for a few days. A place is always left free for an unexpected traveller.

Sweden: the julbord

The Swedish Christmas buffet, the julbord, brings together herring in several sauces, salmon, glazed ham, meatballs, sausages and "Jansson's temptation", a potato and anchovy gratin. It is served with glögg, a hot spiced wine with raisins and almonds.

Mexico: salt cod, pozole and punch

In Mexico, Christmas Eve dinner may include bacalao a la vizcaína, a Spanish inheritance, turkey, romeritos in mole or pozole. During the posadas, from 16 to 24 December, people drink hot fruit punch with sugar cane and tejocotes, and buñuelos with piloncillo syrup are a must.

Philippines: Noche Buena with ham and queso de bola

In the Asian country with the strongest Catholic tradition, Noche Buena (in Spanish) is the great family celebration. After midnight Mass come sweet ham, queso de bola (Edam cheese), lechón and bibingka, a rice cake baked in banana leaves.

Japan: fried chicken and strawberry cake

In Japan Christmas is not a religious holiday, but it has become a celebration for couples and friends. Since the 1970s an advertising campaign has made fried chicken the most popular Christmas dinner, booked weeks in advance. Dessert is "Christmas cake", a sponge with whipped cream and strawberries.
