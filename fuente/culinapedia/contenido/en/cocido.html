After the holidays comes January, with cold weather, tight budgets and a craving for simple food. It is time for spoon dishes, and in Spain few are as complete as cocido. Here is cocido madrileño and its cousins from all over the peninsula.

{fig:img/p/Q1105215.webp|p/cocido-madrileno.html|Cocido madrileño. Photo: Tnarik (CC BY-SA 2.0), Wikimedia Commons.}

A whole meal in one pot

{p:cocido-madrileno|Cocido madrileño} brings together chickpeas, vegetables, meats and sausages in a single pot, slow-cooked for hours. It is served in three «vuelcos» or courses: first the broth, usually with noodles; then the chickpeas with the vegetables; and finally the meats, pork belly, chorizo and morcilla.

From adafina to olla podrida

Its history is long. Many historians link it to the adafina of Sephardic Jews, a chickpea stew left cooking from Friday so as not to light a fire on the Sabbath; hence the custom of serving the pot in several courses. After the forced conversions pork was added, and in the Golden Age the olla podrida became famous, mentioned in Don Quixote. The name «cocido madrileño» appears in the late 19th century, when the dish became a staple of the capital's taverns.

The cocido family

Escudella i carn d'olla. In Catalonia, with a large meatball and thick pasta in the broth.

Cocido montañés. In Cantabria, with white beans and cabbage instead of chickpeas.

Pote asturiano and caldo gallego. Beans, cabbage or turnip greens, potatoes and cured meats.

Cocido maragato. In León, eaten backwards: meats first, then chickpeas, and soup at the end.

Olla gitana. In Murcia, a meatless version with chickpeas, beans, pumpkin and pear.

Puchero andaluz. Lighter, with hen and mint in the broth.

Cheap, with leftovers

One of cocido's virtues is that nothing goes to waste: the leftovers become croquettes, ropa vieja or pringá. We tell you how in {art:sobras|leftover cooking}.

On the side

Bread, pickled chillies and a light white wine or a young red. And afterwards, a good siesta. To keep exploring Spanish cooking, see the {u:paises/espana.html|Spain} pages on Culinapedia.
