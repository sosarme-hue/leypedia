On the night of 5 to 6 January the Three Kings bring presents in Spain and many Latin American countries. But Epiphany also has its own sweets in half the world. Join us for a tour of the 6 January table.

{fig:img/g/Q177166-2.webp|p/roscon-de-reyes.html|Kings' crown cake. Photo: Thomon (CC BY-SA 4.0), Wikimedia Commons.}

Spain: the roscón

The {p:roscon-de-reyes|roscón}, made with brioche dough, orange blossom water and candied fruit, is king of the day, with its hidden figurine and bean. We tell its story in {art:roscon|this article}.

Valencia: the casca de reis

In Valencia and Alicante people give the casca, a ring or serpent of marzipan filled with sweet potato or egg yolk, decorated with colourful icing. It comes in boxes decorated with sweets, chocolate coins and sugared almonds.

France: the galette des rois

In northern France the galette reigns, a disc of puff pastry filled with almond cream, with a figurine (the fève) hidden inside. Whoever finds it becomes king or queen and wears a paper crown. In the south a brioche crown, closer to the Spanish roscón, is preferred.

Portugal: bolo rei and bolo rainha

Bolo rei, with candied fruit and nuts, is eaten from Christmas to Epiphany. Its version without candied fruit, only nuts, is called bolo rainha, «queen cake».

Mexico: rosca de Reyes

The Mexican rosca hides one or more small plastic baby Jesus figures. Whoever finds one must treat everyone to tamales and atole on 2 February, Candlemas. It is served with hot chocolate.

United States: the New Orleans king cake

In Louisiana, the French tradition gave rise to the king cake, a braided cinnamon ring covered in purple, green and gold icing, the colours of Mardi Gras. It is eaten from 6 January until Fat Tuesday.

Greece and Cyprus: vasilopita

Although it is eaten on 1 January, Saint Basil's Day, the principle is the same: a cake with a hidden coin that brings luck for the whole year.

A shared tradition

All these sweets share one idea: sharing a cake as a group and letting luck pick someone. Perhaps that is why they are still so alive. Happy Epiphany!
