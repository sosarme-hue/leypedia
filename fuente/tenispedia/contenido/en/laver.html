Winning all four Grand Slam tournaments in the same year is the hardest feat in tennis. Only five people have done it in singles, and only one has done it twice: the Australian {t:rod-laver|Rod Laver}.

{fig:img/r/Q105550.webp|t/rod-laver.html|Rod Laver. Photo: Joost Evers / Anefo (CC0), Wikimedia Commons.}

The Rockhampton Rocket

Laver was born in 1938 in Rockhampton, Queensland. He was short for a tennis player, just 1.75 metres, but left-handed, very fast and with a huge forearm. His teammates nicknamed him «the Rocket». He was shaped by Harry Hopman in the Australian Davis Cup team that dominated the game, as we tell in {art:davis|the history of the Davis Cup}.

The first Grand Slam, 1962

In 1962, still an amateur, he won the Australian championship, Roland Garros, Wimbledon and Forest Hills in the same year. He was the second man to do it, after {t:don-budge|Don Budge} in 1938. Straight afterwards he turned professional, and for five years he could not play the major tournaments, then reserved for amateurs.

The second, 1969

In 1968 the Open Era began and professionals returned to the majors. The following year, aged 31, Laver did it again against rivals such as {t:ken-rosewall|Ken Rosewall}, {t:tony-roche|Tony Roche} and {t:arthur-ashe|Arthur Ashe}. The last piece came in the 1969 US Open final against Roche, on a wet court where Laver switched to spiked shoes so as not to slip. We tell it in {p:1969-us-open-laver-completes-his-second-grand-slam|this page}.

What we missed

Laver won eleven Grand Slam titles, but missed twenty-one because he was a professional before 1968. Nobody knows how many more he would have won. That is why many still rank him among the greatest players of all time.

His name in Melbourne

Since 2000 the Australian Open centre court has been called Rod Laver Arena, and the Laver Cup, which pits Europe against the rest of the world, has carried his name since 2017. More on {e:era-open|the start of the Open Era}.
