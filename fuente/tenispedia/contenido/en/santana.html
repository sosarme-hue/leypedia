Before Nadal, Alcaraz, Arantxa or Conchita there was a pioneer: {t:manuel-santana|Manolo Santana}. He started out as a ball boy at a Madrid club and went on to win four Grand Slam titles at a time when hardly anyone in Spain played tennis.

{fig:img/r/Q445602.webp|t/manuel-santana.html|Manuel Santana. Photo: Rapsodin (CC BY-SA 3.0), Wikimedia Commons.}

The ball boy

Santana was born in Madrid in 1938 into a humble family. At ten he started working as a ball boy at the Club de Tenis Velázquez, and played with old rackets when the courts were free. A family at the club noticed him and helped him keep studying and training.

The big titles

In 1961 he won Roland Garros, the first major for a Spanish player, and won it again in 1964. In 1965 he won the US championship at Forest Hills, on grass, a surface that was not supposed to suit a clay-court player. And in 1966 came the great success: Wimbledon. His drop shots and lobs, with a flick of the wrist all his own, became famous.

The Davis Cup

Santana led Spain to two Davis Cup finals, in 1965 and 1967, both against mighty Australia. He lost them, but his wins in earlier rounds, such as the 1965 tie in Barcelona against the United States, were followed as national events. Alongside him were players such as {t:andres-gimeno|Andrés Gimeno}, {t:jose-luis-arilla|José Luis Arilla} and, later, {t:manuel-orantes|Manuel Orantes}. More in {art:davis|the history of the Davis Cup}.

The rise of Spanish tennis

Santana made thousands of children want to play tennis and led to courts being built all over the country. Then came Orantes, US Open champion in 1975, and whole generations of champions: {t:arantxa-sanchez-vicario|Arantxa Sánchez Vicario}, {t:conchita-martinez|Conchita Martínez}, {t:juan-carlos-ferrero|Juan Carlos Ferrero}, {t:rafael-nadal|Rafael Nadal} and {t:carlos-alcaraz|Carlos Alcaraz}.

After the racket

After retiring he was Davis Cup captain and director of the Madrid Masters, whose centre court now bears his name. He died in December 2021, and his death was felt across the tennis world.
