Every January, while Europe is in winter, tennis heads to Melbourne in the southern summer. The Australian Open opens the Grand Slam season. Here is the history of the event that was for decades the poor relation of the majors and is now one of the best-loved tournaments on the tour.

{fig:img/a/torneo-australian-open.webp|torneo/australian-open.html|The Australian Open in Melbourne. Photo: Wikimedia Commons.}

The beginnings, 1905

The first Australasian championship was played in 1905 in Melbourne, on grass. For many years it moved between cities: Sydney, Adelaide, Brisbane, Perth and even, twice, New Zealand. From 1972 it settled in Melbourne, at the Kooyong club.

The forgotten major

Australia was far away. Before commercial flights, the sea voyage from Europe took weeks, and many top players did not go. For years it was mostly a tournament for Australians such as {t:rod-laver|Rod Laver}, {t:roy-emerson|Roy Emerson} and {t:margaret-court|Margaret Court}, who won it eleven times. Between 1977 and 1985 it was played in December, and in 1977 it was even held twice. In 1986 there was no tournament, so that it could return to January.

Melbourne Park and hard courts

In 1988 the tournament moved to a new complex, today's Melbourne Park, and swapped grass for hard courts. It was the first major with a retractable roof on its centre court, called Rod Laver Arena since 2000, as we tell in {art:laver|the article on Laver}. From then on, all the stars came back.

Great moments

There Serena Williams won in 2017 while pregnant, as told in {p:2017-australian-open-final-serena-wins-while-pregnant|this page}, and there Djokovic and Nadal played for almost six hours in the 2012 final, the longest in Grand Slam history ({p:2012-australian-open-final-djokovic-v-nadal|page}). {t:novak-djokovic|Novak Djokovic} is the king of the tournament, with ten titles.

The heat

The Australian Open is played in high summer, and temperatures sometimes top 40 degrees. The tournament has a special heat policy that allows roofs to be closed or matches suspended.
