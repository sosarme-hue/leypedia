On 16 December 1775, {a:jane-austen|Jane Austen} was born in Steventon, a village in the south of England. She published four novels in her lifetime, none under her own name, and died at only 41. Two centuries later her books are still read, filmed and argued about as if her characters were people we knew.

{fig:img/r/Q36322.jpg|a/jane-austen.html|Jane Austen. Image: Wikimedia Commons.}

The rector's daughter

Austen was the seventh of eight children of a country clergyman. The family read a great deal and put on plays at home. From her teens she wrote stories and parodies to amuse her family. She never married: she once accepted a proposal and withdrew her acceptance the next day.

«By a Lady»

Her first published novel was {o:sensatez-y-sentimientos|Sense and Sensibility}, in 1811, signed only «By a Lady». Two years later came {o:orgullo-y-prejuicio|Pride and Prejudice}, which she had written under the title «First Impressions» when she was barely past twenty. Then she published {o:mansfield-park|Mansfield Park} and {o:emma|Emma}. Her readers did not know who the author was, although in some circles it was an open secret.

Elizabeth and Mr Darcy

«Pride and Prejudice» opens with one of the most famous sentences in English literature: it is a truth universally acknowledged that a single man in possession of a good fortune must be in want of a wife. The novel tells the story of Elizabeth Bennet, a clever and ironic young woman, and of the proud Mr Darcy, who misjudge each other.

Behind the balls and the visits lies a sharp critique of a society in which women depended on marriage for financial security. Austen tells it with humour, without preaching, and in a style that lets us see what her characters are thinking.

Fame after death

Austen died in Winchester in July 1817. A few months later {o:persuasion|Persuasion} and {o:la-abadia-de-northanger|Northanger Abbey} were published, with a note by her brother Henry that at last revealed her name. Her fame has kept growing ever since.
