Before writing the most famous book in the Spanish language, {a:miguel-de-cervantes|Miguel de Cervantes} was a soldier, a captive, a tax collector and a prisoner. His life reads like an adventure novel, and perhaps that is why he was able to write the greatest of them all.

{fig:img/r/Q5682.jpg|a/miguel-de-cervantes.html|Miguel de Cervantes. Image: Wikimedia Commons.}

Lepanto and Algiers

Cervantes was born in 1547 in Alcalá de Henares. Young and penniless, he joined the Spanish infantry, and in 1571 he fought at the battle of Lepanto, where he was shot twice in the chest and once in the left hand, which he never used again. Hence his nickname, «the one-handed man of Lepanto». He was always proud of that wound.

In 1575, on his way back to Spain, his ship was captured by Barbary pirates. He spent five years as a captive in Algiers, tried to escape four times and was finally ransomed in 1580 by Trinitarian friars.

From tax collector to novelist

Back in Spain he took jobs that had little to do with literature: he requisitioned wheat and oil for the Armada and collected taxes in Andalusia. His accounts got him into trouble and he ended up in prison in Seville. In the prologue to Don Quixote he says the book was conceived «in a prison», and many believe that was the one he meant.

In 1605 he published the first part of {o:don-quijote-de-la-mancha|The Ingenious Gentleman Don Quixote of La Mancha}. It was an instant success: a country gentleman who goes mad from reading too many books of chivalry and rides out to right wrongs with his squire Sancho Panza. The {o:novelas-ejemplares|Exemplary Novels} followed in 1613, and the second part of Don Quixote in 1615.

Why it is the first modern novel

Don Quixote is not just a parody of chivalric romances. Its characters change over the course of the book: Don Quixote grows more sensible and Sancho more of a dreamer. The book also plays with fiction itself: in the second part the characters have read the first, and they even mock a fake sequel published by a certain Avellaneda in 1614.

Cervantes died in Madrid in April 1616, almost at the same time as {a:william-shakespeare|Shakespeare}. That is why 23 April is World Book Day.
