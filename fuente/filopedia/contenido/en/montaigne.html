In 1571, aged 38, a French nobleman called {a:michel-de-montaigne|Michel de Montaigne} withdrew to the tower of his château near Bordeaux. Surrounded by books, he began to write about whatever came into his head, and above all about himself. Without meaning to, he invented a genre: the {g:ensayo|essay}.

A boy who spoke Latin

Montaigne was born in 1533. His father had very particular ideas about education: he had the boy learn Latin before French, with a tutor who spoke to him only in that language. Later he studied law and became a magistrate in the Bordeaux parliament. There he met {a:etienne-de-la-boetie|Étienne de La Boétie}, his great friend. Asked why he loved him so, he gave a famous answer: «Because it was he, because it was I.»

To try, to test, to essay

In French, essai means an attempt or a trial. Montaigne did not set out to write definitive treatises, but to test his judgement on all kinds of subjects: friendship, fear, cannibals, the education of children, death or smells. The first two books of the {o:ensayos|Essays} came out in 1580 and the third in 1588. He kept revising and expanding them until his death.

His models were the classical authors, above all {a:seneca|Seneca} and Plutarch, whom he quotes constantly. What was new was the tone: personal, familiar, unafraid of contradicting itself. «I am myself the matter of my book», he warns the reader.

«What do I know?»

Montaigne lived through the wars of religion between Catholics and Protestants, and he distrusted those who were too sure of being right. His motto was «What do I know?», a form of {g:escepticismo|scepticism} that invites tolerance and caution.

He was mayor of Bordeaux from 1581 to 1585 and died at his château in 1592. His way of writing influenced Shakespeare, Pascal and many others, and whenever someone writes freely about what they think, they follow in his footsteps.
