One day, while marking exam papers, an Oxford professor came across a blank sheet and wrote, without quite knowing why: «In a hole in the ground there lived a hobbit.» That, as he himself told it, was the start of one of the most widely read works of fantasy ever written. The professor was {a:j-r-r-tolkien|J. R. R. Tolkien}, born on 3 January 1892.

Orphan and soldier

John Ronald Reuel Tolkien was born in Bloemfontein, South Africa, but grew up in England, near Birmingham. His father died when he was three and his mother when he was twelve. He became the ward of a Catholic priest, Father Francis Morgan. In 1916 he fought at the Battle of the Somme in the First World War, where nearly all his closest friends were killed. That experience can be felt in his books.

Languages first

Tolkien was a philologist, a specialist in Old English, and taught at Oxford for decades. From childhood he loved inventing languages, and he created several with their own grammar and vocabulary, such as the elvish tongues Quenya and Sindarin. He said he had invented Middle-earth so that his languages would have somewhere to live.

At Oxford he belonged to the Inklings, a group of writer friends who met to read their work aloud. Among them was {a:c-s-lewis|C. S. Lewis}, the author of Narnia, who encouraged him to keep writing.

From the hobbit to the ring

{o:el-hobbit|The Hobbit} was published in 1937 as a children's book and was a success. His publisher asked for a sequel, and Tolkien took more than twelve years to finish it. {o:el-senor-de-los-anillos|The Lord of the Rings} appeared in 1954 and 1955, split into three volumes for publishing reasons, although he conceived it as a single work.

Tolkien died in 1973 without finishing his great project, the history of the Elder Days. His son Christopher put it in order and published it in 1977 as {o:el-silmarillion|The Silmarillion}. Almost all modern fantasy owes him something.
