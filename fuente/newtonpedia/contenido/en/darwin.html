On 24 November 1859 a book with a very long title reached the bookshops of London: On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection. Its author, {p:charles-darwin|Charles Darwin}, had been preparing it for over twenty years. It changed biology for ever.

{fig:img/p/Q1035.webp|p/charles-darwin.html|Charles Darwin. Image: Wikimedia Commons.}

The voyage of the Beagle

In 1831, aged 22, Darwin sailed as naturalist on HMS Beagle, a Royal Navy ship under Captain {p:robert-fitzroy|Robert FitzRoy} that was to chart the coasts of South America. The voyage lasted almost five years. Darwin collected fossils in Patagonia, lived through an earthquake in Chile and, in the Galápagos Islands, noticed that finches, tortoises and mockingbirds differed from island to island.

A dangerous idea

Back in England, Darwin began to suspect that species were not fixed but changed over time. Around 1838 he found the mechanism: each generation more individuals are born than can survive, and those with some advantage leave more offspring. This is {d:natural-selection|natural selection}. But he knew the idea would shock many, and for twenty years he kept gathering evidence without publishing.

Wallace's letter

In 1858 he received a letter from the naturalist {p:alfred-russel-wallace|Alfred Russel Wallace}, then in the Malay Archipelago, who had reached the same idea on his own. Darwin's friends arranged an elegant solution: on 1 July 1858 papers by both men were read to the Linnean Society of London. Darwin then hurried to write an «abstract» of his great work, the book we know.

The book

The first edition sold out to booksellers at once. Curiously, the word «evolution» does not appear in it; the book's last word is «evolved». It sparked great debates, such as the famous one at Oxford in 1860, where {p:thomas-henry-huxley|Thomas Henry Huxley} defended it so fiercely that he became known as «Darwin's bulldog».

After Darwin

Darwin did not know how traits were inherited. The answer came with genetics, and in the 20th century the two were joined in the {d:modern-evolutionary-synthesis|modern evolutionary synthesis}. Today {d:evolution|evolution} is the idea that makes sense of all of biology.
