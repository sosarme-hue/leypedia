A girl turns her head towards us, lips parted, with a huge pearl glowing in the half-light. We do not know who she is, what she was about to say, or whether the pearl was real. Perhaps that is why Girl with a Pearl Earring has become one of the best-loved paintings in the world.

{fig:img/m/Q185372.jpg|b/johannes-vermeer.html|Johannes Vermeer, Girl with a Pearl Earring (around 1665). Mauritshuis, The Hague.}

A portrait that is not a portrait

{b:johannes-vermeer|Johannes Vermeer} painted her around 1665 in Delft. It is not a commissioned portrait but a tronie: the Dutch name for studies of a head with a striking expression or costume, sold as works of art in their own right. The blue and yellow turban, exotic at the time, was part of the costume.

Who was the model?

Nobody knows. Maria, the painter's eldest daughter, who would have been about twelve or thirteen, has been suggested, as has Magdalena van Ruijven, daughter of his patron. Tracy Chevalier's novel and the 2003 film imagined a maid called Griet, but that is pure fiction.

The pearl that may not have been one

The pearl is painted with just two touches: a white highlight at the top and the reflection of the collar below. It does not even have a hook to hang from the ear. Given its size, some historians think it was varnished glass or polished tin, since a natural pearl that big would have cost a fortune.

What science found

In 2018 an international team studied the painting with X-rays, scanners and microscopes. They found that the background, now black, was originally a green curtain whose pigment has degraded over the centuries, and that Vermeer had painted tiny eyelashes invisible to the naked eye. The blue of the turban is ultramarine, made from Afghan lapis lazuli and more expensive than gold.

A painter almost forgotten

Vermeer died in 1675, ruined and with eleven children. Only about thirty-five of his paintings survive, and for two centuries hardly anyone remembered his name. The French critic Théophile Thoré-Bürger rediscovered him around 1860 and called him "the sphinx of Delft". Girl with a Pearl Earring sold at auction in The Hague in 1881 for two guilders and thirty cents.

Where to see it

Today it hangs in the Mauritshuis in The Hague and rarely travels. If you visit the Netherlands, the {m:rijksmuseum|Rijksmuseum} in Amsterdam holds other Vermeer treasures, such as The Milkmaid.
