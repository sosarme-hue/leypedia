It is the most famous painting in the world and also one of the smallest in its room: 77 by 53 centimetres. Every year millions of people queue at the {m:museo-del-louvre|Louvre} to see it for a few seconds behind bulletproof glass. We start the year with ten curiosities about {b:leonardo-da-vinci|Leonardo da Vinci}'s Mona Lisa.

{fig:img/m/Q12418.jpg|b/leonardo-da-vinci.html|Leonardo da Vinci, Mona Lisa (around 1503-1519). Louvre Museum, Paris.}

1. She has a name

The sitter was Lisa Gherardini, wife of a Florentine silk merchant, Francesco del Giocondo. Hence the names La Gioconda and Monna Lisa ("Madam Lisa").

2. Leonardo never delivered it

The commission came around 1503, but Leonardo kept retouching it for years and took it with him to France, where he died in 1519.

3. That is why it is in Paris

King Francis I, who had welcomed the painter, acquired the picture. It hung at Fontainebleau and Versailles and, after the Revolution, went to the Louvre. Napoleon even kept it in his bedroom.

4. The smile moves

Leonardo used sfumato, extremely thin layers of paint that blur outlines. The corners of the mouth and eyes are left in shadow, so the smile seems to appear and disappear depending on where we look.

5. She has no eyebrows

Or no longer has them. A high-resolution analysis published in 2007 found traces of an eyebrow, which may have been lost in old cleanings.

6. It was stolen in 1911

An Italian employee, Vincenzo Peruggia, walked out with it hidden under his smock. The theft was world news and, paradoxically, made the painting far more famous. It turned up in Florence two years later.

7. Picasso was a suspect

During the investigation the poet Guillaume Apollinaire was arrested, and {b:pablo-picasso|Picasso} was questioned. Both were released.

8. It has been attacked

It has had acid, a stone, a teacup and, in 2022, a cake thrown at it. That is why it is protected by special glass.

9. The landscape does not match

Look closely and the horizon on the left is lower than on the right. It is an imaginary landscape of paths, bridges and mountains fading into mist.

10. There is another Mona Lisa in the Prado

The {m:museo-del-prado|Prado Museum} owns a copy painted at the same time in Leonardo's workshop, probably by one of his pupils. A 2012 restoration revealed its original background, and today it helps us imagine the colours of the Paris version before it darkened.

And to look at it calmly, if you ever get close, try {art:mirar|our ten-minute method}.
