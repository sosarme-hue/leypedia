Her gaze turned to stone anyone who dared to look at her, and instead of hair she had snakes. {d:medusa|Medusa} is the most famous monster in Greek mythology. But her story is more tragic than it seems, and in recent years she has become a very different kind of symbol.

{fig:img/p/Q160730.webp|d/medusa.html|Medusa, painted by Caravaggio on a wooden shield around 1597. Uffizi Gallery, Florence. Image: Public domain}

A mortal Gorgon

According to Hesiod's {l:teogonia|Theogony}, Medusa was one of the three Gorgons, with her sisters {d:esteno|Stheno} and Euryale. The three lived at the western edge of the world, but only Medusa was mortal. That difference was her undoing.

From maiden to monster

The Roman poet Ovid tells another version in his {l:las-metamorfosis|Metamorphoses}. Medusa had been a very beautiful young woman, famous above all for her hair. {d:poseidon|Poseidon} raped her inside the temple of {d:atenea|Athena}, and the goddess, offended by the sacrilege, punished Medusa rather than the god: she turned her hair into snakes. Many modern readers see in this story an injustice that still resonates.

Perseus and the shield

The hero {d:perseo|Perseus} was sent to bring back her head. With the help of the gods he obtained winged sandals, a sickle, a helmet of invisibility and a polished bronze shield. Thanks to the shield he could see Medusa's reflection without looking at her directly, and he cut off her head while she slept. From her neck sprang the winged horse {d:pegaso|Pegasus} and the warrior Chrysaor.

A head that protects

Perseus used the head to turn his enemies to stone and later gave it to Athena, who set it on her shield, the aegis. The Gorgon's face, the gorgoneion, was painted on shields, vases and doors for centuries as a charm against evil.

From the Renaissance to today

Caravaggio painted her on a shield with a frozen scream, and Benvenuto Cellini sculpted Perseus holding up her head in Florence. Today Medusa is also the logo of a well-known fashion house and, for many people, a symbol of women punished for what others did to them.
