A serpent covered in the green feathers of the quetzal bird. That is how {d:quetzalcoatl|Quetzalcoatl} was depicted, one of the most important gods of Mesoamerica, worshipped for centuries by very different peoples. God of the wind and of knowledge, and creator of humankind, he is still surrounded by mystery.

{fig:img/p/Q179818.webp|d/quetzalcoatl.html|Quetzalcoatl, the Feathered Serpent. Photo: Eddo · CC BY 3.0}

A very ancient god

The feathered serpent already appears in Teotihuacan, the great city of central Mexico, almost two thousand years ago. Its temple is decorated with huge serpent heads surrounded by feathers. Much later the Maya worshipped it as Kukulcan in Yucatán, and the K'iche' of Guatemala as {d:gucumatz|Gucumatz}, one of the creator gods of the {l:popol-vuh|Popol Vuh}.

Creator of human beings

According to Aztec myths, the world had been created and destroyed several times. Before the Fifth Sun, the present one, there were no people left. Quetzalcoatl went down to Mictlan, the underworld, to recover the bones of earlier generations. He had to outwit {d:mictlantecuhtli|Mictlantecuhtli}, the lord of the dead. He escaped with the bones, ground them and sprinkled them with his own blood. From that mixture today's humanity was born.

Rival of Tezcatlipoca

His great rival was {d:tezcatlipoca|Tezcatlipoca}, the «smoking mirror», god of the night and of fate. The two worked together to create the world, but they also clashed again and again. In one myth, Tezcatlipoca got Quetzalcoatl drunk, and he, ashamed, left his city.

The priest-king of Tula

Legends also tell of a priest-king of Tula who bore the god's name. After his fall he went away to the east and, according to some accounts, promised to return. For a long time it was said that the emperor Moctezuma mistook Hernán Cortés for the returning Quetzalcoatl. Today many historians believe that idea was constructed after the conquest.

Wind and morning star

Quetzalcoatl took many forms. As {d:ehecatl|Ehecatl} he was the wind god, who sweeps the way for the rain. As {d:tlahuizcalpantecuhtli|Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli} he was the planet Venus, the morning star. He was also credited with inventing the calendar, writing and the growing of maize.

The serpent of Chichén Itzá

At the great pyramid of Kukulcan in Chichén Itzá, during the spring and autumn equinoxes, the afternoon light draws a wavy shadow on the staircase that looks like a serpent sliding down towards the carved head at the base. Every year thousands of people come to see it.
