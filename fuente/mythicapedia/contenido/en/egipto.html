Egyptian religion had hundreds of gods, many with animal heads and names that changed over three thousand years of history. To find your way around, it helps to know the main ones. These are the most important gods of ancient Egypt.

{fig:img/p/Q1252904.webp|d/ra.html|Ra, the sun god, in an Egyptian depiction. Image: Public domain}

Ra, the sun

{d:ra|Ra} was the sun god and one of the most revered. Every day he crossed the sky in his boat and every night he travelled through the underworld, where he fought the serpent of chaos, Apophis. The pharaohs presented themselves as his sons. In time he merged with {d:amon|Amun}, the god of Thebes, to become Amun-Ra.

Osiris, Isis and Horus

This is Egypt's great myth. {d:osiris|Osiris}, a good king, was murdered and cut into pieces by his brother {d:set|Set}. His wife {d:isis|Isis} searched for the pieces, put them together and brought him back to life just long enough to conceive their son {d:horus|Horus}. Osiris went on to rule the world of the dead, and Horus, the falcon-headed god, defeated Set and became the protector of the pharaohs.

Anubis and the judgement of the dead

{d:anubis|Anubis}, with the head of a jackal, was the god of mummification. In the judgement described in the {l:libro-de-los-muertos|Book of the Dead}, he weighed the heart of the deceased on a scale against the feather of {d:maat|Maat}, the goddess of truth and order. {d:thot|Thoth}, the ibis-headed god of writing, recorded the result. If the heart was heavier than the feather, it was devoured by the monstrous {d:ammit|Ammit}.

Goddesses of home and love

{d:hathor|Hathor}, sometimes shown as a cow or with horns, was the goddess of love, music and joy. {d:bastet|Bastet}, the cat goddess, protected the home, and in her city, Bubastis, thousands of cats were mummified in her honour.

Sky and earth

The Egyptians pictured the sky as the goddess {d:nut|Nut}, her body arched over the earth, who was her husband {d:geb|Geb}. Every night Nut swallowed the sun and every morning she gave birth to it again. And in Memphis people worshipped {d:ptah|Ptah}, the craftsman god who created the world through speech.

For a few years the pharaoh Akhenaten tried to impose the worship of a single god, the sun disc {d:aton|Aten}. After his death, Egypt went back to its old gods.
