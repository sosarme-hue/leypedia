Banting and insulin: the first patient, January 1922

12 January 2027 · 6 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

In Toronto a 14-year-old boy received the first insulin injection. Diabetes was no longer a death sentence.

On 11 January 1922, at Toronto General Hospital, a 14-year-old boy named Leonard Thompson was given an injection of pancreatic extract. He weighed about 30 kilos and was dying of diabetes. It was the first time insulin had been used in a human being.

A disease without hope

Before insulin, type 1 diabetes was a death sentence, especially for children and young people. The only treatment was a near-starvation diet that prolonged life by a few months or a few years. Since the late nineteenth century it had been known that the pancreas was involved, but nobody had managed to isolate the substance that controlled blood sugar.

An idea in the middle of the night

Frederick Banting was a young Canadian surgeon with no research experience. In 1920, after reading an article, he had an idea for extracting that substance from the pancreas. He persuaded Professor John Macleod of the University of Toronto to lend him a laboratory for the summer, and a student, Charles Best, was assigned as his assistant. Working with dogs, they obtained an extract that lowered blood sugar.

The first patient

The first injection given to Leonard Thompson did not work well: the extract was impure and caused a reaction. The biochemist James Collip improved the purification, and on 23 January the boy received a second dose. His blood sugar fell and he began to recover. He lived for another thirteen years.

A Nobel in record time

In 1923, barely two years after the experiments, Banting and Macleod received the Nobel Prize in Medicine. Banting, annoyed that Best had not been included, shared his part of the prize with him, and Macleod did the same with Collip. The researchers sold the patent to the University of Toronto for one dollar each, so that insulin could reach everyone. Today 14 November, Banting's birthday, is World Diabetes Day.
