The Balmis Expedition: the vaccine that crossed the ocean in 22 children

24 November 2026 · 6 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

On 30 November 1803 the first international vaccination campaign in history set sail from A Coruña.

On 30 November 1803 the corvette María Pita set sail from the port of A Coruña. On board were the doctor Francisco Javier Balmis, from Alicante, several assistants, the head of an orphanage and 22 orphan boys. In their arms, the children carried the smallpox vaccine. It was the Royal Philanthropic Vaccine Expedition, the first international vaccination campaign in history.

The problem: keeping the vaccine alive

A few years earlier Edward Jenner had discovered the vaccine against smallpox, and the news soon reached Spain. King Charles IV, whose family had suffered from the disease, backed the idea of taking it to Spain's territories in the Americas and Asia. But there was a problem: at the time there was no way to keep the vaccine viable on a voyage of several weeks at sea.

The solution: a human chain

The solution was as ingenious as it was harsh. Two children were vaccinated; nine or ten days later, when the sores appeared, material was taken from them to vaccinate two more, and so on for the whole crossing. The children were, quite literally, the vaccine's means of transport. They were cared for by Isabel Zendal, head of the A Coruña orphanage, who travelled with her own son.

From the Americas to the Philippines

The expedition called at the Canary Islands, Puerto Rico and Venezuela. There it split: the deputy director, José Salvany, headed south towards New Granada, Peru and Bolivia, on an exhausting journey on which he would eventually die. Balmis went to Cuba and Mexico, and from Acapulco he crossed the Pacific to the Philippines with 26 more Mexican children. He then reached Macao and Canton, and returned to Spain in 1806 after sailing round the world.

An example for history

It is estimated that the expedition made it possible to vaccinate hundreds of thousands of people, and it left vaccination boards in many cities. Jenner himself praised it. In 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic, Isabel Zendal's name was back in the news, and the Spanish army's operation against the pandemic was called Operation Balmis.
