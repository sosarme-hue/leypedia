«Snow White», Disney's first feature

15 December 2026 · 5 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

In December 1937 Walt Disney bet his studio on a feature-length cartoon. It paid off.

In the mid-thirties, Hollywood called it «Disney's Folly». Walt Disney had set his heart on making a feature-length cartoon, something nobody in America had tried, and he had gone deep into debt to do it. On 21 December 1937 Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs premiered. The folly paid off.

From shorts to a feature

Disney had become famous with shorts a few minutes long, above all with Mickey Mouse, who first appeared in 1928, and with the Silly Symphonies series. But shorts made little money, and Walt wanted to tell longer stories, with characters able to move audiences the way flesh-and-blood actors did.

Three years of work

Production took about three years and involved hundreds of artists. To give depth to the backgrounds, the studio developed the multiplane camera, which placed several layers of artwork at different distances. The animators studied how real people and animals moved, and the budget soared to almost one and a half million dollars, an enormous sum at the time.

A historic success

The premiere was a triumph. Audiences wept at Snow White's apparent death and learned songs like Whistle While You Work by heart. The film became one of the highest-grossing of its day and allowed Disney to build a new studio in Burbank.

In 1939 the Academy gave him a very special honorary Oscar: a full-size statuette accompanied by seven little ones, one for each dwarf.

The start of a tradition

After Snow White came Pinocchio, Fantasia, Dumbo and Bambi, and later Peter Pan or Mary Poppins, which mixed actors and animation. Almost ninety years on, every new animated release still owes something to that risky bet of 1937.
