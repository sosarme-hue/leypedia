It is probably the most recognisable classical music in the world: it plays in adverts, lifts, weddings and films. But {b:antonio-vivaldi|Antonio Vivaldi}'s Four Seasons are much more than a catchy tune. They are one of the first examples of music that tells a story without words, and "Winter", now approaching, is perhaps the most original of the four.

Four concertos and four sonnets

Vivaldi, the "red priest" because of the colour of his hair, was a priest and violin teacher at the Ospedale della Pietà in Venice, an orphanage whose girls formed one of the most admired orchestras in Europe. Around 1725 he published in Amsterdam a set of twelve violin concertos entitled Il cimento dell'armonia e dell'inventione, "The contest between harmony and invention". The first four are The Four Seasons.

Each concerto comes with a sonnet, perhaps written by Vivaldi himself, and the lines are printed in the score next to the music that depicts them. It is a very modern idea: the composer tells us exactly what to imagine at each moment.

What to listen for in each season

  • Spring. Birds sing (three solo violins trilling), streams flow and a brief storm arrives. In the second movement a shepherd sleeps while the viola imitates the barking of his dog.
  • Summer. Oppressive heat, the song of the cuckoo and the turtle dove, and at the end a violent storm that destroys the crops. It is the most dramatic season.
  • Autumn. The harvest festival, peasants dancing and drinking until they fall asleep, and a hunt at dawn.
  • Winter. The concerto begins with repeated notes that imitate chattering teeth in the cold. The second movement is one of Vivaldi's most beautiful melodies: we sit by the fire while the rain falls outside, and the violins' pizzicati are the drops. In the last, someone walks carefully on the ice, slips and falls.

From oblivion to best-seller

After Vivaldi's death his music was forgotten for almost two centuries. Its revival began in the twentieth century and took off with recordings: by the middle of the century The Four Seasons had become a best-seller, and the work became the gateway to classical music for millions of people.

Four ways to hear them

  • The Romantic one: {b:anne-sophie-mutter|Anne-Sophie Mutter} recorded them as a young woman with {b:herbert-von-karajan|Herbert von Karajan} and the Berlin Philharmonic, with a large orchestra and a velvety sound.
  • The virtuoso one: {b:itzhak-perlman|Itzhak Perlman} offers a warm, brilliant reading, among the most popular for decades.
  • The revolutionary one: {b:fabio-biondi|Fabio Biondi} and Europa Galante, on period instruments, recorded them in the early 1990s with extreme tempos, violent contrasts and surprising effects. For many it was like hearing the work for the first time.
  • The re-imaginings: the Argentine Astor Piazzolla wrote his own Four Seasons of Buenos Aires in tango rhythm, and the composer Max Richter published a "recomposition" of Vivaldi's work in 2012. Both are good ways to return to the original with fresh ears.

A tip

Look for an edition that includes the sonnets and read them as you listen. You will discover that every bark, every storm and every slip is written in the score. And when you finish, move on to {b:johann-sebastian-bach|Bach}'s violin concertos: he admired Vivaldi so much that he transcribed several of his concertos for keyboard.
