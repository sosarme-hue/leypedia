    The Geminids, the best meteor shower of the year

    8 December 2026 · 5 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

    On the night of 13 to 14 December the most abundant shooting stars arrive. When and how to see them.

    August's Perseids are the most famous shooting stars, but not the most plentiful. That title usually goes to the Geminids, which peak every year on the night of 13 to 14 December. Under a clear, dark sky you can see more than a hundred meteors an hour.

    What shooting stars are

    Shooting stars are not stars. They are specks of dust, most of them smaller than a grain of sand, that enter the atmosphere at great speed. Friction with the air heats them so much that they burn up, leaving a brief trail of light about a hundred kilometres up.

    A meteor shower happens when Earth passes through a cloud of debris left along its orbit by a comet or an asteroid. Because all the particles arrive from the same direction, they seem to come from a single point in the sky, the radiant. For the Geminids, the radiant lies in the constellation Gemini, near its star Castor.

    An unusual origin

    Most meteor showers come from comets. The Geminids, by contrast, come from an asteroid called Phaethon, discovered in 1983, which passes very close to the Sun and sheds dust. Its meteors are fairly slow and bright, and sometimes yellowish.

    How to watch them

    • Find a spot far from city lights, with a clear horizon.
    • Geminids can be seen from mid-evening, because Gemini rises early, but the best time is after midnight, when the radiant is high.
    • You don't need a telescope or binoculars: just lie back and look at a wide patch of sky.
    • Let your eyes adapt to the dark for about twenty minutes and don't look at your phone.
    • Wrap up warm: December nights are very cold.

    This year the Moon will be a waxing crescent and will set early, so it won't get in the way in the second half of the night. Check other showers of the year on our meteor showers page and the best advice in getting started with stargazing.
