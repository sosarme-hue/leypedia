    Voyager 1, one light-day from Earth

    19 November 2026 · 5 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

    The most distant spacecraft ever built reaches a symbolic distance these days: its signal takes 24 hours to reach us.

    Almost half a century ago a spacecraft the size of a small car lifted off from Cape Canaveral. Today it is the most distant human-made object in existence, and around now it reaches a symbolic milestone: one light-day. Light, and the radio signals it sends, now take about 24 hours to travel from it to Earth. It is Voyager 1.

    A journey using a rare alignment

    The two Voyager probes were launched in 1977. They took advantage of an alignment of the outer planets that happens only once every 175 years and that allowed them to hop from one to the next, using each planet's gravity as a boost. Voyager 1 flew past Jupiter in 1979 and Saturn in 1980. There it swung close to Titan, Saturn's giant moon, and was flung out of the solar system.

    A pale blue dot

    On 14 February 1990, about 6 billion kilometres away, the probe turned its cameras around and photographed Earth. In the picture our planet is a tiny dot, smaller than a pixel, suspended in a ray of sunlight. The astronomer Carl Sagan, who had suggested taking the photo, wrote that this dot was our home, the place where everyone we have ever heard of had lived.

    In interstellar space

    In August 2012 Voyager 1 crossed the heliopause, the boundary where the influence of the solar wind ends, and entered interstellar space. It was the first spacecraft ever to do so. Voyager 2 followed in 2018.

    The probe carries a golden record with sounds and images of Earth: greetings in 55 languages, music from around the world, whale song and the sound of rain. It is a message in case anyone ever finds it.

    A spacecraft slowly powering down

    Voyager 1 runs on a generator that turns the heat of plutonium into electricity, and every year it produces a little less. To save power, engineers have been switching off instruments one by one. One day it will stop transmitting, but it will keep travelling in silence for millions of years. Discover other missions on our space probes page and get a feel for the distances in the universe.
