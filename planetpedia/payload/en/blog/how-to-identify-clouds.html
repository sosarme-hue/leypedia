    How to identify clouds and tell what the weather will do

    1 December 2026 · 5 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

    Cumulus, stratus, cirrus and cumulonimbus: a simple guide to reading the sky.

    Watching clouds is not just a pastime: with a little practice, the sky tells us what the weather will do in the next few hours. Learning to recognise the main types of cloud is easier than it seems.

    How a cloud forms

    Air always carries water vapour, which is invisible. When that air rises it cools, and the vapour condenses into tiny water droplets or ice crystals around specks of dust or salt. Millions of those droplets together make a cloud.

    The names of clouds

    In 1802 an English pharmacist and amateur meteorologist, Luke Howard, proposed naming clouds with Latin words, as was done with plants. His system is still used today: cirrus means curl, cumulus heap, stratus layer and nimbus rain cloud. Combining them gives the ten cloud genera recognised by the World Meteorological Organization.

    A quick guide

    • Cirrus: thin and white, like brushstrokes high in the sky. They are made of ice. If they spread and the sky gradually clouds over, a front with rain may arrive within a day or two.
    • Cumulus: cotton-wool clouds with flat bases. Small, separate ones mean fair weather.
    • Cumulonimbus: huge towers that can rise more than ten kilometres and end in an anvil shape. They bring thunderstorms, lightning, heavy showers and sometimes hail.
    • Stratus: a low, grey, even layer that hides the sun. It can bring drizzle. When it touches the ground, we call it fog.
    • Altocumulus: small puffs or ripples at mid height, the «mackerel sky». The old saying «mackerel sky, not long dry» warns that they often come before a change in the weather.
    • Nimbostratus: a thick, dark layer that brings steady rain or snow for hours.

    A tip for practising

    Go out one day and look at the sky, note which clouds you see and check the next day what the weather did. After a few weeks of practice you will start making your own forecasts.
