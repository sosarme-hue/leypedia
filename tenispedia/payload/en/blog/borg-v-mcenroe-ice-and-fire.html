    Borg v McEnroe: ice and fire

    19 November 2026 · 6 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

    The shortest and most intense rivalry in tennis history, from the 1980 Wimbledon final to the Swede's retirement.

    One was Swedish, blond, cold as ice, with a headband and the same expression on every point. The other was a New Yorker, left-handed, brilliant and unable to keep quiet in front of an umpire. Björn Borg and John McEnroe played each other only fourteen times, yet their rivalry is still one of the most famous in history.

    Two opposite styles

    Borg played from the baseline with a two-handed backhand and a topspin few could return. His calm was so striking that he was nicknamed «Ice Borg». McEnroe came to the net, touched the ball with the softest of hands and argued with umpires using a line that became famous: «You cannot be serious!». They were order and chaos, and the public could not choose.

    The 1980 Wimbledon final

    Borg came into that final with four Wimbledon titles in a row. McEnroe was 21. In the fourth set they played a twenty-minute tie-break that McEnroe won 18-16, after saving five match points. It looked as if the Swede would collapse, but he won the fifth set 8-6 and his fifth straight Wimbledon. Many consider it the best match in the tournament's history; we tell it in this page.

    1981: the changing of the guard

    A year later McEnroe took his revenge: he beat Borg in the Wimbledon final and, weeks later, in the US Open final. Borg, who had won the French Open six times, never managed to win in New York. After that final he left the court without waiting for the ceremony. Soon afterwards he quit the tour at just 26.

    A perfect tie

    Their head-to-head ended at seven wins each. The rivalry lasted barely three years, but it changed tennis: it brought millions of viewers to television and turned players into stars. In time the two became friends, and they even inspired a film, Borg vs McEnroe (2017).
