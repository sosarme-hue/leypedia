    The Davis Cup: the story of the Salad Bowl

    19 November 2026 · 6 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

    How a challenge between Harvard students in 1900 became the great team competition of tennis.

    At the end of November, when the rest of the tour is already resting, the world's best national teams play for the Davis Cup. It is the oldest team competition in international sport, and its story begins with a few students and a silver bowl.

    A challenge between students

    In 1899 four Harvard University players decided to challenge the British, who then ruled tennis. One of them, Dwight F. Davis, paid out of his own pocket for a trophy from a Boston silversmith: a large silver bowl that Spanish speakers would soon call the Ensaladera, the Salad Bowl. The first tie was played in August 1900 at the Longwood Cricket Club, near Boston, between the United States and the British Isles. The Americans won, and Davis was on the team.

    The trophy that brought nations together

    In its early years the Cup was a duel between the United States and Britain. Then came Australasia, which joined Australia and New Zealand, and France, whose «Four Musketeers» won six times in a row between 1927 and 1932. For many players, winning the Davis Cup mattered as much as winning Wimbledon. One of the most famous matches is Don Budge against Gottfried von Cramm in 1937, told in this page.

    The Australian reign

    Between 1950 and 1967 Australia won the Cup fifteen times. Their captain, Harry Hopman, shaped a generation of champions such as Frank Sedgman, Lew Hoad, Ken Rosewall, Roy Emerson and Rod Laver. Spain reached two finals in that era, in 1965 and 1967, led by Manolo Santana, and lost both to the Australians.

    Spain, champions

    Spain's first Salad Bowl came in 2000, at the Palau Sant Jordi in Barcelona, against Australia; we tell the story in the page on that final. Then came 2004, 2008, 2009, 2011 and 2019, several with Rafael Nadal as the leading figure. Six titles in all.

    From the classic format to the Finals

    For more than a century the Cup was played in knockout ties throughout the year, at the home of one of the two teams and over the best of five matches, with atmospheres that sometimes felt like football. In 2019 it moved to a format with a final stage at a single venue at the end of the season. The change still has supporters and critics, but Davis's idea, that tennis is also played for a country, lives on.

    Curiosities

    More than a bowl. The original bowl had no room for so many names, so a tray and later a tiered base were added.

    The women's version. Since 1963 there has been the Federation Cup, now the Billie Jean King Cup.
