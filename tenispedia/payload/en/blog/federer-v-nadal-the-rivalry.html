    Federer v Nadal: the rivalry that changed everything

    5 January 2027 · 6 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

    Forty matches, nine Grand Slam finals and a friendship: the story of «Fedal».

    For almost twenty years Roger Federer and Rafael Nadal played each other forty times, nine of them in Grand Slam finals. Their rivalry, which fans called «Fedal», is probably the most famous in modern tennis.

    The artist and the warrior

    Federer, Swiss and five years older, played with natural elegance: a one-handed backhand, a precise serve, shots that seemed effortless. Nadal, a left-hander from Mallorca, was pure intensity, with a heavy topspin forehand that bounced very high, right at Federer's backhand. Their styles were opposites, like Borg and McEnroe before them.

    The first duels

    They first met in Miami in 2004, and Nadal, aged 17, won. From 2005 Nadal dominated on clay, beating Federer in four Roland Garros finals, while Federer reigned on grass. In 2007 they even played an exhibition on a court that was half grass and half clay, the «Battle of Surfaces», told in this page.

    Wimbledon 2008

    Federer was chasing a sixth straight Wimbledon. Nadal won the first two sets, Federer the next two, and the fifth ended 9-7 to the Spaniard almost in darkness, after nearly five hours of play and two rain delays. Many consider it the greatest match ever; we tell it in this page.

    Melbourne 2009 and 2017

    In the 2009 Australian Open final Nadal won again, and Federer broke down in tears at the ceremony. Eight years later, in 2017, both were returning from injury and met in another Melbourne final: Federer won in five sets, in one of the most unexpected comebacks in sport.

    The numbers

    Nadal won 24 of the 40 matches and six of the nine Grand Slam finals. Federer retired in 2022 at the Laver Cup in London, playing doubles with Nadal; the image of the two of them crying hand in hand went around the world. Nadal said goodbye in 2024 at the Davis Cup.
