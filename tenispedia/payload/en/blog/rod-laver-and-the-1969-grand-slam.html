    Rod Laver and the 1969 Grand Slam

    24 November 2026 · 5 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

    The only man to win all four majors in the same year twice.

    Winning all four Grand Slam tournaments in the same year is the hardest feat in tennis. Only five people have done it in singles, and only one has done it twice: the Australian Rod Laver.

    The Rockhampton Rocket

    Laver was born in 1938 in Rockhampton, Queensland. He was short for a tennis player, just 1.75 metres, but left-handed, very fast and with a huge forearm. His teammates nicknamed him «the Rocket». He was shaped by Harry Hopman in the Australian Davis Cup team that dominated the game, as we tell in the history of the Davis Cup.

    The first Grand Slam, 1962

    In 1962, still an amateur, he won the Australian championship, Roland Garros, Wimbledon and Forest Hills in the same year. He was the second man to do it, after Don Budge in 1938. Straight afterwards he turned professional, and for five years he could not play the major tournaments, then reserved for amateurs.

    The second, 1969

    In 1968 the Open Era began and professionals returned to the majors. The following year, aged 31, Laver did it again against rivals such as Ken Rosewall, Tony Roche and Arthur Ashe. The last piece came in the 1969 US Open final against Roche, on a wet court where Laver switched to spiked shoes so as not to slip. We tell it in this page.

    What we missed

    Laver won eleven Grand Slam titles, but missed twenty-one because he was a professional before 1968. Nobody knows how many more he would have won. That is why many still rank him among the greatest players of all time.

    His name in Melbourne

    Since 2000 the Australian Open centre court has been called Rod Laver Arena, and the Laver Cup, which pits Europe against the rest of the world, has carried his name since 2017. More on the start of the Open Era.
