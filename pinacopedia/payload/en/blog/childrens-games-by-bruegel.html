Children's Games: how children played 500 years ago, according to Bruegel

29 December 2026 · 6 min read · By Alejandro Rosàs

More than eighty children’s games in a single painting. How many are still played today?

Christmas holidays, children at home, games in the street. In 1560 Pieter Bruegel the Elder painted a picture devoted entirely to them: Children's Games, now in the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna. In a village square more than two hundred children play some eighty different games. It is almost an encyclopedia of play in the Renaissance.

Games we still know

Many will sound familiar. There are children playing blind man's buff, leapfrog, knucklebones, spinning tops, hoops, stilts and handstands. Others play marbles, dolls or swings, and a few blow soap bubbles through a pipe. One group holds a pretend wedding, another a procession, and a boy rides a hobby horse.

Games we have lost

Other games are no longer played, such as blowing up a pig's bladder to make a ball. Historians have identified many of them by comparing the painting with texts of the time, and some are still debated.

Only children?

One detail is unsettling: many of these children have adult faces, serious, almost old. That is why some historians think the painting is also an allegory: adults behaving like children, busy with games of no importance. Like Netherlandish Proverbs, it would be a portrait of the "world upside down".

A game to play at home

Open the picture full screen and search with the children: who is playing blind man's buff? where is the boy on stilts? how many spinning tops are there? It is a great way to look at a painting carefully, and to discover that five centuries ago children played almost the same games as now.

If you enjoy it, Bruegel has other crowded pictures, such as The Hunters in the Snow, with its skaters, or The Fight Between Carnival and Lent.
