Cats and Christmas trees: how to stop them knocking it over (and getting hurt)

Cats love to climb the tree and play with the decorations. Ideas to keep the tree standing and protect your cat from decoration hazards.

Key points
  • Use a heavy, stable base, or anchor the tree to the wall or ceiling.
  • Put unbreakable ornaments low and glass ones high up or elsewhere.
  • Avoid tinsel and ribbon: if swallowed they are very dangerous.
  • Protect light cables and unplug the lights when you go out.
  • If the tree is real, cover the water in the stand.
  • Offer alternatives: a tall cat tree and daily play.

A tree that stays up

Choose a wide, heavy base. If your cat is a keen climber, tie the tree with nylon thread to a hook in the wall or ceiling. Place it away from furniture the cat could jump from, and in a room you can close when nobody is home. Some owners prefer a small tree on a table, or set it up a few days early without decorations so the cat loses interest.

Safer decorations

Glass ornaments break and can cut paws or mouths. Hang only unbreakable decorations made of fabric, wood or plastic on the lower branches, and fix them firmly with closed wire rather than loose hooks.

Tinsel, ribbon and string are among the most dangerous items: if a cat swallows them, they can tangle in the intestine and cause a blockage that needs surgery. It is best not to use them. Do not leave edible decorations, such as chocolate figures, within reach of pets either.

Lights, water and plants

Run cables through protectors or behind furniture, and unplug the lights when you go out or go to bed, to prevent chewing and electric shocks. If the tree is real, cover the water in the stand: it may contain resin, preservatives or fertiliser. Sweep up fallen needles often, as they irritate the mouth and stomach. And be careful with poinsettia, mistletoe and holly, which are toxic to cats.

Give your cat alternatives

A bored cat finds its own entertainment. Spend a few minutes a day playing with a wand toy or a ball, and put a tall cat tree nearby where it can watch from above. If it heads for the tree, distract it with a toy rather than scolding it. Citrus-scented deterrents at the base can help with some cats.

When to see a vet

If your cat has swallowed tinsel, ribbon or string, do not pull on anything showing from the mouth or bottom; go to the vet. Also go if it vomits, will not eat or seems lethargic after chewing cables, plants or decorations.

General guidance. It does not replace a visit to your vet.
