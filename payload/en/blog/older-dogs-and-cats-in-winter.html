Older dogs and cats in winter: cold-weather care

With age, cold weather takes a bigger toll on joints and energy. How to help your older dog or cat through winter in comfort.

Key points
  • Cold weather makes arthritis pain worse.
  • A soft, raised bed away from draughts helps a lot.
  • Shorter, more frequent walks are better than one long one.
  • Watch their weight: with less exercise they easily put it on.
  • Have vet check-ups at least once a year, ideally every six months.

Why the cold affects them more

Many older dogs and cats have arthritis, although cats hide it very well. In cold, damp weather their joints stiffen and hurt more. They also regulate their temperature less well and have less muscle. Signs include struggling to get up, limping when they start walking, not wanting to climb stairs or, in cats, no longer jumping up to high places.

A more comfortable home

Provide a thick or memory-foam bed, slightly raised off the floor and away from doors and windows. Put rugs on slippery floors, ramps or steps for the sofa or bed and, for cats, a low-sided litter tray, with food and water on the same floor where they spend the day. Do not put the bed too close to heaters or radiators: an older animal may not move away in time and could get burned.

Exercise and weight

Short, frequent walks keep joints moving without tiring them. Warm up the muscles by walking slowly for the first few minutes, dry them well when you get back and use a coat if they feel the cold. With less activity they easily gain weight, and every extra kilo loads the joints: adjust portions and weigh your pet now and then.

Check-ups

From around seven or eight years of age, depending on species and size, a vet check-up at least once a year is recommended, with blood and urine tests. That way common age-related problems such as kidney disease, diabetes or thyroid disease are picked up early.

When to see a vet

If your older pet limps, moves less, drinks or urinates more than usual, loses weight or changes in character, book an appointment: there are treatments for arthritis pain that greatly improve quality of life. Never give human painkillers such as ibuprofen or paracetamol, which can be very toxic.

General guidance. It does not replace a visit to your vet.
