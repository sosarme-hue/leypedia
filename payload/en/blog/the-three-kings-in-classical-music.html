The Three Kings in classical music

From the medieval drama of the star to Amahl, the first opera for television, by way of Bach, Bizet, Cornelius and Respighi.

By Alejandro Rosàs Navarro

Share:FacebookWhatsApp

On the night of 5 January, in Spain and many Spanish-speaking countries, the Three Kings bring the presents. Melchior, Caspar and Balthazar have inspired painters, poets and musicians for centuries. Here is a journey through the music they have inspired, from the Middle Ages to an opera written for television.

The star in the Middle Ages

In medieval cathedrals, short sung liturgical dramas known as the Officium stellae, or "Office of the Star", were performed at Epiphany. Clerics played the Magi, followed a star hung in the church and conversed with Herod in plainchant. They were one of the seeds of European theatre and opera. In the same period the Auto de los Reyes Magos, one of the earliest plays in Castilian Spanish, was written in Toledo.

Bach: the end of the Christmas Oratorio

The last two cantatas of Bach's Christmas Oratorio tell of the Magi's arrival in Jerusalem and their adoration of the Child. The sixth, intended for 6 January, ends with trumpets and timpani in one of Bach's most brilliant finales. We tell you all about it in our Christmas Oratorio guide.

Bizet and the "March of the Kings"

In Provence, a popular carol, the Marche des Rois, has been sung for centuries to accompany the Magi on their way to the stable. Georges Bizet used it in 1872 in his incidental music for the play L'Arlésienne, and today that solemn march is one of the best-known themes in the suites from the work.

Peter Cornelius: "The Kings"

The German composer Peter Cornelius included in his Christmas Songs a piece entitled "Die Könige", "The Kings". As the singer narrates the journey of the Magi, the piano plays the Lutheran chorale "How brightly shines the morning star". It is one of the loveliest Christmas songs of the Romantic era, and its composer, as it happens, was born on 24 December.

Respighi: the Adoration in a Botticelli painting

In 1927 Ottorino Respighi wrote the Trittico botticelliano, three pieces for chamber orchestra inspired by paintings by Botticelli. The central one, "The Adoration of the Magi", opens with a bassoon evoking an ancient, Eastern-flavoured melody and quotes the Advent hymn Veni, veni, Emmanuel. Colourful, delicate music, ideal for Epiphany morning.

Menotti: an opera for television

The most curious story is that of Gian Carlo Menotti. In 1951 the American network NBC commissioned him to write an opera to be broadcast on Christmas Eve. Menotti could not find a subject until, walking through the Metropolitan Museum in New York, he stopped in front of an Adoration of the Magi by Hieronymus Bosch. So Amahl and the Night Visitors was born: the story of a poor, lame boy who welcomes the Three Kings into his home on their way to Bethlehem. It was the first opera written for television, and for years it was broadcast every Christmas in the United States. It lasts under an hour and is perfect to watch as a family.

For the night of the Kings

My suggestion: Bach's sixth cantata while dinner is being prepared, Bizet's "March of the Kings" to welcome them, and Amahl with the children before bed. And with this we close the blog's Christmas series. Thank you for keeping us company over the holidays.
