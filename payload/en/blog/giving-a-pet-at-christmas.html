Giving a dog or cat as a Christmas present? What to think about first

A pet is not a toy: it lives 10 to 20 years and needs time, space and money. If the whole family is sure, how to do it well, and alternatives to a surprise under the tree.

Key points
  • A dog or cat is a commitment of 10 to 20 years.
  • The decision must be made by the whole family, never as a surprise for another household.
  • Work out the yearly cost: food, vet, insurance and care.
  • The holidays, with noise and guests, are not the best time for a new arrival.
  • Consider adopting from a shelter.
  • An alternative: give the collar, the bed or a book, and choose the animal together afterwards.

A commitment of many years

A dog lives on average 10 to 15 years, depending on breed and size, and a cat can live beyond 15. Throughout that time it needs food, daily walks or play, veterinary care, vaccinations, parasite control and company. Holidays also need planning: who will look after it, or whether it can travel with the family.

Every year after the holidays, shelters take in animals that were given without much thought. That is why many vets and charities ask people to make the decision calmly.

Questions before deciding

The whole family wants the animal and knows who will take care of each task. There is time every day to walk, play and train. The home and the rental agreement allow it. Nobody in the house is allergic. The yearly cost can be met, which with food, vet, insurance and extras usually amounts to several hundred euros a year at least.

In Spain, the animal welfare law also requires animals to be microchipped and, for dogs, liability insurance and an owner training course.

If you are sure: how to do it well

It is better for the animal to arrive a few days before or after the holidays, when the house is calm. Noise, visitors and changes of routine are very stressful for a puppy or kitten.

Consider adoption: shelters have dogs and cats of all ages, already vaccinated and identified, and staff will help you choose the one that best suits your family. If you buy, do so from a registered breeder and never through adverts with no guarantees.

An alternative to the surprise

Instead of an animal under the tree, give what it will need: a collar, a bed, a carrier or a book about the breed, with a card saying you will choose it together. The excitement is the same and the decision is made calmly.

When to see a vet

When the animal arrives, book a vet appointment within the first week for a general check-up, a vaccination and parasite-control plan, and to check or fit the microchip.

General guidance. It does not replace a visit to your vet.
