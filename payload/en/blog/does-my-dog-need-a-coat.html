Does my dog need a coat in cold weather?

Not every dog needs a coat, but some appreciate one a great deal. Which dogs feel the cold most, how to tell, and how to look after them in winter.

Key points
  • Small, short-haired, very lean, young and old dogs feel the cold most.
  • Nordic breeds with a double coat rarely need one.
  • Shivering, hunching or refusing to walk are signs of feeling cold.
  • A coat should cover the back and chest without rubbing or restricting movement.
  • Dry your dog well after rain and check its paw pads.

Which dogs feel the cold most

Small and toy breeds, such as the Chihuahua or Yorkshire Terrier, lose heat easily. So do dogs with very short hair or no undercoat, such as Greyhounds, Whippets, Boxers or Pinschers, and very lean dogs. Puppies and older dogs regulate their temperature less well, as do dogs with conditions such as heart or thyroid problems or arthritis.

On the other hand, Nordic or mountain breeds with a double coat, such as the Husky, Malamute or St Bernard, are built for the cold, and a coat can make them too hot.

How to tell if your dog is cold

The most common signs are shivering, hunching up, holding the tail low, lifting paws off the ground, looking for shelter or refusing to keep walking. Very cold ears and paws are also a clue.

How to choose a coat

It should cover the back and chest, fit snugly without being tight, and let your dog walk, run and relieve itself comfortably. Measure back length and chest girth before buying. For rain, a waterproof fabric is best; for dry cold, a lined one. Take it off indoors so your dog does not overheat.

Other winter care

Dry your dog thoroughly after a rainy walk, especially between the toes. Check the paw pads: cold, snow and road salt can crack them. If you walk where there is salt or antifreeze, wash its paws when you get home, as these are irritating and toxic if licked. Shorten walks in very cold weather, but do not skip them, and keep its bed away from draughts.

When to see a vet

See your vet if your dog shivers a lot even when wrapped up, seems lethargic, limps in the cold, or has cracked or bleeding paw pads. Older dogs with arthritis often get worse in winter and may need their treatment adjusted.

General guidance. It does not replace a visit to your vet.
