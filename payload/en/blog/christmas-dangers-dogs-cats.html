Christmas dangers for dogs and cats: food, decorations and plants

Chocolate, leftovers from the roast, tinsel, poinsettias and visitors: the holidays bring plenty of temptations and some real risks for your dog or cat. What to watch for and what to do if they eat something they shouldn't.

Key points
  • Keep chocolate, Christmas sweets, grapes, raisins and sugar-free sweets out of reach.
  • Do not give cooked turkey or chicken bones or fatty leftovers from the roast.
  • In homes with cats, avoid tinsel and ribbons, and do not bring lilies indoors.
  • Protect cables and secure the tree.
  • Set up a quiet corner for when guests arrive.
  • Keep your vet's number and that of an emergency clinic open on public holidays to hand.

Festive food

Chocolate is one of the most dangerous foods for dogs, and for cats too. It contains theobromine, which their bodies eliminate very slowly. The purer and darker the chocolate, the more theobromine it contains: dark chocolate and baking chocolate are the most dangerous. Watch out for chocolate selection boxes, truffles and chocolate decorations hanging on the tree.

Grapes and raisins can cause acute kidney failure in dogs, and no amount is known to be safe. Christmas pudding, mince pies, fruit cake and stollen are full of dried fruit and should be kept well away from dogs.

Sugar-free sweets and chewing gum may contain xylitol, a sweetener that causes a sudden drop in blood sugar in dogs and can damage the liver. Some nuts, such as macadamia nuts, are also toxic to dogs.

Stuffing, gravy and many side dishes contain onion, garlic or leek, which damage the red blood cells of dogs and cats. Very fatty leftovers, such as turkey skin or pigs in blankets, can cause vomiting and diarrhoea and even pancreatitis.

Bones and leftovers from the roast

Cooked bones, especially poultry bones, splinter easily. They can get stuck in the mouth or oesophagus or perforate the intestine. Do not give them to your pet, and put leftovers in a closed bin or out of reach, because many dogs know how to raid the rubbish.

Also watch out for kitchen string from the roast, cocktail sticks and foil with meat juices on it: they smell delicious and can cause a blockage if swallowed.

Decorations, tree and presents

Tinsel, gift ribbon and string are especially dangerous for cats: they love to play with them and, if swallowed, they can cause a serious intestinal blockage. If you have a cat, it is best to do without them.

Glass baubles break and cut, and fairy-light cables can cause electric shocks if chewed. Secure the tree so it cannot topple over if the cat climbs it and, if it is a real tree, cover the water in the stand, which may contain preservatives or resin.

Button batteries from toys and remote controls are very dangerous if swallowed, as they can burn the oesophagus or stomach in a short time. Tidy away presents and wrapping as soon as possible.

Christmas plants

The poinsettia is an irritant: if chewed it can cause drooling, vomiting or diarrhoea, although the effects are usually mild. Mistletoe and holly are more toxic and can cause more serious digestive problems, especially their berries.

Lilies, common in flower arrangements, are very dangerous for cats: chewing a leaf or drinking water from the vase is enough to damage the kidneys. If you have a cat, do not bring them into the house. You can find more information in our guide to toxic plants for cats.

Guests, noise and changes of routine

The holidays change routines and fill the house with people and sometimes noise. Many dogs and cats find this hard. Set up a quiet corner with their bed, water and a toy, in a room where guests do not go. Ask visitors, especially children, not to feed them from the table.

Try to keep to their usual feeding and walking times. And if fireworks are let off in your area on New Year's Eve, bear in mind that many animals are afraid of them: walk your dog before they start, close the windows and check that the ID tag and microchip details are up to date in case your pet escapes.

When to see a vet

If you think your dog or cat has eaten something toxic, call your vet or an emergency clinic straight away, even if there are no symptoms yet: many treatments work better the sooner they are given. Keep the wrapper or note what and how much was eaten. Do not make your pet vomit yourself or give home remedies without veterinary advice. Also seek urgent help if your pet vomits repeatedly, has a swollen abdomen, trembles, has seizures or seems very unwell.

General guidance. It does not replace a visit to your vet.
