Classical music for Christmas: 12 essential works

From Schütz to Britten: twelve pieces of classical music for the holidays, with the story behind each one.

By Alejandro Rosàs Navarro

Share:FacebookWhatsApp

Christmas has inspired composers for centuries: masses for the night of the 24th, oratorios for the feast days, carols that the whole world now sings and even a ballet that has become a family tradition. This is a personal selection of twelve works to accompany the holidays, arranged roughly by period. Each one links to its composer's biography on Classicapedia, in case you want to know more about who wrote it.

1. Heinrich Schütz: The Christmas Story (1660)

Heinrich Schütz, the great German master of the seventeenth century, wrote this "Christmas history" in Dresden. An Evangelist narrates the story, and the characters (the angels, the shepherds, the Magi and Herod) appear with small groups of voices and instruments. It is intimate music of surprising freshness, and one of the seeds of the great oratorios that came later.

2. Marc-Antoine Charpentier: Midnight Mass (around 1690)

Marc-Antoine Charpentier built this mass on noëls, the French popular carols that everyone knew. The result is a joyful, almost dance-like mass in which the congregation of the time recognised the songs of their childhood. It is perfect for Christmas Eve.

3. Arcangelo Corelli: Concerto grosso Op. 6 No. 8, "Christmas Concerto" (published 1714)

The title page bears the words fatto per la notte di Natale, "made for Christmas night". Arcangelo Corelli ends the work with a "Pastorale" in a gentle rocking rhythm that evokes the shepherds watching by the manger. About fifteen minutes of serene, luminous music, ideal for Christmas morning.

4. Johann Sebastian Bach: Christmas Oratorio (1734)

It is in fact six cantatas, written for the feast days from Christmas to Epiphany in Leipzig. Bach opens the first with timpani and trumpets in a jubilant chorus, "Jauchzet, frohlocket!" ("Shout for joy, exult!"). If you only have half an hour, listen to the first cantata. If you have an afternoon, listen to all six: it is one of the peaks of sacred music.

5. George Frideric Handel: Messiah (1741)

Essential. We have written a complete listening guide, with its history and recommended recordings. Handel's biography also tells how a German composer became England's national musician.

6. Franz Xaver Gruber: Silent Night (1818)

The most widely sung carol in the world was born in Oberndorf, near Salzburg, on Christmas Eve 1818. The priest Joseph Mohr had written the words, and the schoolteacher and organist Franz Xaver Gruber set them for two voices, choir and guitar. It was sung that same night, and from there it travelled around the world with touring singers.

7. Adolphe Adam: "Minuit, chrétiens" (1847)

Adolphe Adam, composer of the ballet Giselle, wrote this Christmas song to a poem by Placide Cappeau. Known in English as O Holy Night, it is a favourite of great tenors and sopranos at their Christmas concerts.

8. Hector Berlioz: L'enfance du Christ (1854)

A "sacred trilogy" about the flight into Egypt, delicate and little known to the wider public. It comes with a delightful story: Berlioz first presented the chorus "The Shepherds' Farewell" as the work of an imaginary seventeenth-century composer, Pierre Ducré, to tease the critics who disliked his modern music. Audiences and critics applauded it.

9. Camille Saint-Saëns: Christmas Oratorio (1858)

Saint-Saëns wrote it in his early twenties, when he was an organist in Paris, and tradition has it that he composed it in just a few days. It is an intimate work for soloists, choir, organ and strings, with melodies of simple beauty. A wonderful alternative for anyone who already knows Bach and Handel by heart.

10. Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky: The Nutcracker (1892)

The Christmas ballet par excellence. The story of Clara, the nutcracker and the Land of Sweets was first performed in St Petersburg in December 1892. Tchaikovsky had discovered a new instrument in Paris, the celesta, and used it in the "Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy", whose tinkling sound is now inseparable from the season.

11. Engelbert Humperdinck: Hansel and Gretel (1893)

Humperdinck's opera was first performed in Weimar two days before Christmas 1893, conducted by a young Richard Strauss. Ever since, it has been the traditional Christmas opera in German-speaking countries. The children's "Evening Prayer" is one of the tenderest melodies in the repertoire.

12. Benjamin Britten: A Ceremony of Carols (1942)

Britten wrote these carols for treble voices and harp during his sea voyage from the United States to England, in the middle of the war. They set medieval English texts to twentieth-century music that is both simple and radiant. Listen to it with a good children's choir.

To go further: two twentieth-century gems

If you want more, look for the Four Motets for Christmas by Francis Poulenc, for unaccompanied choir, and La Nativité du Seigneur by Olivier Messiaen, nine meditations for organ that take Christmas into a completely new world of sound.

And to end the holidays, there is nothing like the Vienna New Year's Concert, whose history and curiosities we tell you about.
