A new puppy at home: how to prepare for the first days

If a puppy is joining your home these days, a practical guide to help it settle in: what to prepare, the first night, the vet and first lessons.

Key points
  • Get the bed, bowls, collar or harness, lead and toys ready beforehand.
  • Keep feeding the same food it was eating at first.
  • On the first night, put its bed near you.
  • See the vet within the first week.
  • Do not take it out on the street until your vet says so, but do socialise it at home.
  • Reward it for toileting in the right place.

Before it arrives

Have ready a bed, water and food bowls, a light collar or harness with an ID tag, a lead, suitable chew toys and puppy pads. Check your home at puppy height: cables, cleaning products, toxic plants, shoes and anything small it could swallow. Agree family rules, such as whether it may get on the sofa, so everyone behaves the same way.

The first days and the first night

At first, keep feeding what it ate with the breeder or shelter and, if you want to change it, do so gradually over a week. Give it time to explore calmly and avoid crowds of visitors. On the first night it will often cry because it misses its mother and littermates: putting its bed near your bedroom, with an item of clothing that smells of you, helps it feel less alone.

The vet

Book an appointment within the first week. The vet will give it a general check-up, check or fit the microchip, set up the vaccination and deworming schedule and tell you when it can safely go out on the street, usually one or two weeks after the last puppy vaccination. Remember to register it on your region's animal register.

First lessons

Take it to toilet in the same place when it wakes up, after eating and after playing, and reward it as soon as it goes. If it has an accident, clean up without scolding. Teach it its name and to come to you with treats and short games. Even if it cannot go out yet, you can socialise it at home: let it get to know sounds, calm people and other healthy, vaccinated dogs. The period from 3 to 14 weeks of age is the key stage for socialisation.

When to see a vet

See the vet if your puppy has diarrhoea or vomiting, will not eat, is lethargic or coughs. In an unvaccinated puppy these signs can be caused by serious diseases such as parvovirus or distemper.

General guidance. It does not replace a visit to your vet.
