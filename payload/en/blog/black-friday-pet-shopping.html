Black Friday for your dog or cat: what is worth buying

The November sales are a good time to stock up on what your pet always uses. What is worth buying, what is not, and how to avoid fake deals.

Key points
  • Buy what you already use: the same food, litter and parasite treatments.
  • Check the expiry date before buying large bags.
  • Compare with prices from previous weeks to spot fake discounts.
  • Carriers, beds, cat trees and harnesses are often well discounted.
  • Do not switch food suddenly just because another brand is cheaper.

What is most worth buying

The products your dog or cat always uses: its usual dry or wet food, cat litter, poop bags and the parasite treatments your vet has recommended. Buying several months at once at a discount is a real saving.

Durable items are also often on sale: carriers, beds, cat trees, harnesses, leads, water fountains and bowls. If you were planning to buy one, now is a good time.

Be careful with large bags

An open bag of dry food loses freshness and aroma over the weeks, and the fats can go rancid. Work out how much your animal eats per month and do not buy more than it will eat before the best-before date. Keep the food in its original bag, tightly closed, inside an airtight container in a cool place.

How to spot fake deals

Note the price of what you usually buy a few weeks beforehand. Some shops raise prices before the sales so the discount looks bigger. Compare the price per kilo, not just per pack, and watch the delivery costs. Buy from well-known shops and be wary of very low prices on parasite treatments, as fakes do circulate.

What not to buy on impulse

A different food just because it is cheaper: food changes should be made gradually over a week to avoid diarrhoea. Poor-quality toys that break into small pieces. And, of course, never an animal: a dog or cat is not a bargain.

When to see a vet

Before bulk-buying a prescription diet, a supplement or a new parasite treatment, check with your vet that it is right for your pet's age, weight and health.

General guidance. It does not replace a visit to your vet.
