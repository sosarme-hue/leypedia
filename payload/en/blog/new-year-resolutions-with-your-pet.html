New Year resolutions with your pet: more walking, a healthier weight

A few simple resolutions to start the year that improve your pet's health and your own: move more, manage weight and catch up on routine care.

Key points
  • Surveys suggest more than half of dogs and cats seen by vets are overweight.
  • Check whether you can feel its ribs and see a waist.
  • Weigh food portions and count the treats.
  • Add one walk or ten minutes of play a day.
  • Put vaccinations, parasite control and the annual check-up in the calendar.

Is your pet overweight?

Run your hand along its ribs: you should feel them easily, without pressing, but not see them. From above you should see a waist behind the ribs, and from the side the belly should tuck up towards the back legs. If you cannot feel the ribs or see a waist, your pet is probably carrying extra weight. Your vet can confirm this and work out its ideal weight.

Eating better

Weigh the daily portion on kitchen scales rather than measuring by eye with a cup. Treats should be no more than 10% of what it eats per day; you can use some of its kibble as treats, or pieces of carrot for dogs. Avoid table scraps. If weight needs to come off, do it gradually with a plan from your vet, especially with cats, for whom sudden fasting can cause liver problems.

Moving more

For dogs, make the walk a bit longer, change routes so they can sniff new places, or try activities such as agility or scent work. For cats, two or three play sessions of five to ten minutes with a wand toy, and puzzle feeders that make them work for their food. It is a resolution that is good for you too.

Catching up on routine care

Put the dates for vaccinations, internal and external parasite treatments and the annual check-up in your calendar. Check that the microchip details are up to date with your phone number and address and, for dogs, that liability insurance is in force. Brush its teeth or ask your vet about alternatives.

When to see a vet

Before starting a diet or a more intense exercise plan, especially if your pet is older or has a health condition, talk to your vet.

General guidance. It does not replace a visit to your vet.
