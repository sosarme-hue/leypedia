Gifts for dogs and cats: safe and useful Christmas ideas

Gift ideas your pet will actually use, how to choose safe toys for its size, and what to avoid.

Key points
  • The best gift is time: a long walk or a play session.
  • Choose toys of the right size that cannot be swallowed whole.
  • Sniffing toys and treat-dispensing toys keep pets busy for a long time.
  • For cats: wand toys, cat trees, boxes and tunnels.
  • Avoid cooked bones, rope that frays and toys with small parts.

Ideas for dogs

Treat-dispensing toys made of tough rubber, filled with food, keep dogs busy and calm. Snuffle mats and puzzle toys stimulate their sense of smell, their main sense. A good harness, a new lead, an orthopaedic bed for an older dog or a coat for one that feels the cold are useful gifts.

Ideas for cats

Cats love to hunt: a feather wand or a fabric mouse that you play with is better than lots of toys left lying on the floor. A tall cat tree, a window hammock, a tunnel or a simple cardboard box provide plenty of fun. A water fountain can encourage them to drink more.

How to choose a safe toy

Size matters: the toy should be bigger than the animal's mouth so it cannot be swallowed. Choose tough materials and check them often for damage. Remove broken toys, small balls for large dogs and soft toys with eyes or buttons that can be pulled off. Put away toys with string, rope or feathers when you are not there to supervise.

What is best not to give

Cooked bones, because they splinter. Human treats, especially chocolate, grapes or raisins and anything containing xylitol. Costumes that are tight or restrict movement. And too much food: a special treat is fine, but leftovers from Christmas dinner can cause vomiting, diarrhoea or pancreatitis.

When to see a vet

If your dog or cat swallows part of a toy and then vomits, will not eat, has a painful belly or stops passing stools, see your vet: it may be a blockage.

General guidance. It does not replace a visit to your vet.
