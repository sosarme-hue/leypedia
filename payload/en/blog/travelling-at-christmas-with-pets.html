Travelling at Christmas with your dog or cat: car, train, plane and accommodation

If you are spending the holidays away, what to prepare when travelling with your pet: documents, carrier, the rules for each type of transport and what to pack.

Key points
  • Take the health record or passport, with microchip and vaccinations up to date.
  • To leave Spain for another EU country you need an EU pet passport and rabies vaccination.
  • In the car, your pet must be secured with a harness, carrier or barrier.
  • Train and airline companies have weight and size limits: book in advance.
  • Check that the accommodation accepts pets and whether there is a supplement.
  • Bring its food, water, bed and a familiar toy.

Documents

Your pet must be microchipped and up to date with vaccinations. Within Spain the health record is usually enough, although some regions require rabies vaccination. To travel to another EU country you need an EU pet passport, with the rabies vaccine given at least 21 days before travel. Some countries, such as Ireland, Finland or Malta, also require a tapeworm treatment for dogs.

By car

Your pet must be secured: with a harness clipped to the seat belt, in a carrier that is firmly fixed, or in the boot behind a dog guard. That way it does not distract the driver and is protected if you brake hard. Stop every two hours or so for water and a leg stretch, and never leave it alone in the car. If it gets travel sick, give a light meal a few hours before setting off rather than just before.

By train and plane

In Spain, the national rail operator Renfe allows small dogs and cats in a carrier on many trains, and in recent years has extended admission to larger dogs on some routes with their own ticket. Conditions and prices change, so check when booking.

On planes, each airline sets its own rules: animals of up to about 8 kg in a carrier usually travel in the cabin, and larger ones go in the hold. Places are limited, so book them as soon as you have your ticket. Get your pet used to the carrier beforehand.

What to pack and accommodation

Bring its usual food for every day, water, bowls, its bed or a blanket that smells of home, lead, bags, a familiar toy and a small first-aid kit. For cats, a folding litter tray. Get written confirmation that the hotel, holiday home or relatives' house accepts the animal and whether there is a supplement or size limit. If you are staying with someone, plan a quiet spot for your pet, away from the noise of family dinners.

When to see a vet

Book a vet appointment a few weeks ahead if you need the EU pet passport, rabies vaccine or tapeworm treatment. Also ask if your pet gets travel sick or very anxious on journeys: there are solutions.

General guidance. It does not replace a visit to your vet.
